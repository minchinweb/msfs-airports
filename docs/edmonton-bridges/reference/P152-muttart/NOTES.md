# P152 Muttart stop — modeling brief

Edmonton. Valley Line Southeast, low-floor Flexity, under catenary. At grade. Opened with the line on 4 November 2023. Centre used by the project: 53.53621, -113.48000. That point is the stop node in `lrt-tracks.json`.

The stop sits at the bottom of the south ramp off the Tawatinâ Bridge. The ramp is in the R14 notes. This stop belongs with that ramp. It is not a downtown street stop, and it is not P157 Davies. Davies is the elevated station on T127. The code is P152. It is not a bridge, so it has no R code and no T code.

Numbers below are cited. Where two sources disagree, both are given. A line in "Still missing" has no published as-built figure in the sources checked. Nothing here was filled by adding one citation to another. A reference-design grade was not turned into an elevation.

## Platform

**What it is.** Two side platforms, one for each track. The project agreement table (Schedule 5 Part 5, 8 February 2016) lists Muttart as at grade, side-loading, neighbourhood canopy. Wikipedia says side-loading platforms, surface, two tracks. The City's accessibility page names a northbound platform and a southbound platform, with a Muttart Plaza entrance on the northbound platform and a Muttart Service Road entrance on both. It is not a centre platform.

**Length.** No surveyed length was found. Two system figures, and two mapped lengths, are published. They were not reconciled.

The agreement says a platform shall be long enough for the longest train at maximum service, and shall not be less than 90 m. The City's April 2022 article says the maximum length of a two-car low-floor train is 90 m (295 ft), and that this length is reflected in the length of the platforms on Valley Line Southeast stops. That article is about the line. It is not a measurement of this platform.

OpenStreetMap, read 14 October 2026, draws two closed platform rings. They are not in `lrt-tracks.json`. The west ring, way 1157283683, has long sides of 103.8 m and 103.8 m. The east ring, way 1157283685, has long sides of 103.6 m and 103.6 m. Those are horizontal lengths of the mapped sides. They are not a survey. The 90 m minimum and the mapped long sides are both kept.

**Width.** No surveyed width was found. The agreement says a side-loading platform shall be at least 4.0 m from the trackside edge to the outside curb, barrier, sidewalk, or building face. Each mapped end is two short sides. Those sides were not added together to make a width.

**Height.** No surveyed height of this platform was found, above the rail or above the sidewalk. The agreement says the platform height, measured from the top of the closest rail, shall not exceed 360 mm. That is a maximum. It is not a statement that this platform is 360 mm high.

The system figures are different statements, and they are not a height of this stop:

- City, April 2022: inside a low-floor train you are 0.3 m off the ground, slightly higher than a typical sidewalk curb. Inside a high-floor train you are approximately 1 m (3.3 ft) off the ground. Those are the train floors.
- City accessibility page: Valley Line Southeast trains offer sidewalk-level boarding at all stops and stations. There is no step up or down.
- Project agreement, Schedule 5 Part 7: the vertical difference between the vehicle floor at the door and the platform shall not exceed 50 mm, and the horizontal gap shall not exceed 75 mm.

The 0.3 m, the 360 mm maximum, and the 50 mm gap were not added together. No elevation of the platform above sea level was found. The vertical datum is missing. Phone GPS altitudes in the photograph files were not used.

**Cross-fall.** The agreement says the platform shall slope down and away from the track, between 1.25% and 2.50%. No as-built cross-fall was found.

**Canopy and shelter.** The agreement assigns this stop the Contemporary Park theme and a neighbourhood canopy. A neighbourhood canopy, in that agreement, has a metal roof, a wood soffit, a curved profile in section, and one row of steel columns at the non-track edge of a side platform, at least 3000 mm on centre. The underside of a stop canopy shall be at most 3.8 m above the platform. Each side platform shall have at least 125 m² under canopy. Those are requirements.

The same agreement requires at least four glass shelters on each side platform, under the canopy, each at least 6.5 m², with a short interior side of at least 1.6 m. The City's April 2022 article says the stop shelters have benches, room for wheelchairs and strollers, and are heated. That is the city's description of Valley Line stops. A count of the shelters at this stop was not taken from the photographs.

The 24 September 2026 photograph shows both platforms, a wood soffit, glass shelters under the roof, a yellow tactile edge, and the rails in a concrete slab. The 21 May 2022 photograph, taken after the stop was built and before the line opened, shows the curved canopy from outside and a leaf sculpture on the roof. The Edmonton Arts Council describes that sculpture, Best Fronds, as two sections on canopies diagonal from each other. Each section is 30 feet long and 4 feet high. Those feet are the artwork. They are not the canopy.

**Catenary.** The line runs under catenary. The 24 September 2026 photograph shows the contact wires and a pole standing between the two tracks. The agreement does not allow a pole on a platform taller than 4.5 m from the top of the platform to the top of the pole. No count of poles at this stop was found.

**Track.** Two tracks. Gauge 1,435 mm is the figure Structurae gives for the Tawatinâ Bridge, and the nearby mapped crossover is tagged `gauge=1435`. Neither is a separate measurement at this platform. The 2013 assessment, written as reference design, said the river-valley track would be direct fixation rather than embedded track. The 2026 photograph shows a concrete slab between the platforms. The slab in the photograph and the words "direct fixation" were not treated as one surveyed detail.

## Approaches

The north approach is the ramp down from the bridge. The south approach is the line leaving toward Strathearn. Gradients and elevation changes for the built approaches were not found.

**North, from the bridge.** The project agreement calls the south abutment the transition from the bridge to the ramp, with a vertical face perpendicular to the track, immediately north of this stop. The same agreement says the height of the ramp immediately north of Muttart stop shall be no more than 6 m, measured from adjacent grade to top of rail. That is a maximum. It is not an as-built height, and it is not an elevation above sea level. The agreement also says the visual mass of that ramp shall be reduced by landscaped earth, tree screening, and texture on the ramp walls.

The R14 notes give the mapped run from the end of the bridge tag, at 53.5374, to the mapped stop at 53.53621, -113.48000. The west track is 199.1 m and the east track is 213.1 m, clipped from ways 1280982479 and 1280982480. Those lengths are cited from that file. They were not recomputed here. They are the mapped run to the stop. They are not a measured ramp, and they are not the point where the ramp meets grade.

The 2013 assessment says the stop and its northern approach sit on a steep grade. From the 98 Avenue bridge the line was then shown descending on a pile-supported elevated approach, then a fill-supported approach, then the stop. Five retaining walls were then conceived, RW-01 through RW-05, 120 m to 230 m long and 2.5 m to 6 m high. The same assessment's 98 Avenue drawing labels the descent beyond the crossing as −5.600% and then −0.275%. The R14 notes already record those labels, and they record that the drawing says the grades are approximate. They are reference design. The elevation numbers on that drawing were not transcribed. The 6 m ramp maximum and the 2.5 m to 6 m wall range are different sentences. They were not merged.

The 21 May 2022 photograph shows a low concrete wall beside the walk to the platform. No height was read from it. The 10 August 2020 photograph looks north from the stop while the stop was still track slab. The alignment rises toward the river bridge. The pylons are in the distance. That is the ramp during construction.

The stop is south of 98 Avenue. The tracks at the platform are in their own right of way. They are not in a traffic lane of 98 Avenue. 98 Avenue is crossed on the elevated guideway, which is part of R14. The agreement requires an access road from the Muttart service road across the trackway, south of the stop, to the conservatory access roads.

The agreement also requires stairs from grade to the north access of the west platform. The photographs saved here do not show that stair.

**South, toward Strathearn.** The next stop is Strathearn, 53.53171, -113.46231, in `lrt.md`. The distance between those centres was not computed.

The 2013 assessment says the track climbs out of the valley on Connors Hill, parallel to Connors Road. The agreement's drainage section treats the trackway from the south end of the Tawatinâ Bridge to the top of Connors Hill as one run, and this stop is on that run. No as-built grade for the climb was found. The Kâhasinîskâk Bridge is the pedestrian bridge over Connors Road. It is not this stop.

In the 2026-10-14 extract the west-track surface route passes through the stop node and continues south-southwest, then the longitude turns back toward the east. A surface crossover of 39 m, the length stored in `lrt-tracks.json`, lies south of the stop. It is not a platform. The 2013 assessment also conceived a storage track parallel to the northwest side of the stop, for a breakdown in the valley, not for long-term storage. That siding was not found as its own way in the extract.

A sign in the 24 September 2026 photograph points to Connors Road. The conservatory glasshouses are behind the left-hand platform in that frame. The glass pyramids are not in it.

## What reads from the air

No openly licensed aerial of this stop was found.

From the ground, the stop is a concrete platform slab, a curved canopy with a wood soffit, and glass shelters, in the trees at the bottom of the ramp. The 2013 assessment said the stop was designed to be simple and visually unobtrusive. The pyramids of the Muttart Conservatory are the object that reads. They are in scope with this stop.

At 1,000–3,500 ft AGL the pyramids and the ramp off the bridge are the picture. The stop itself is a platform slab and a low canopy in the trees. It is not a station building. If the south ramp is modelled, this is the slab at the bottom of it.

## Conservatory

The glass pyramids are in scope with this stop. They are B254, the Muttart Conservatory, including the support greenhouses. They are not an observatory, and they are not a bridge. The Journal states 24 m from floor to apex for the tropical and temperate pyramids, and 18 m for the arid and feature pyramids. The City's arid-pyramid page states 59 feet. The support greenhouses sit between this stop and those pyramids. They are part of B254, not a second building code. The photographs are in `B254-conservatory/photos/`. The figures are in `B254-conservatory/NOTES.md`.

## Still missing

- Surveyed platform length. The requirement is at least 90 m. The mapped long sides are 103.8 m and 103.6 m.
- Surveyed platform width. The requirement is at least 4.0 m for a side platform.
- Surveyed platform height above the rail, and above the sidewalk. The requirement is a maximum of 360 mm above the closest rail. The 0.3 m figure is the low-floor train, system-wide.
- As-built cross-fall. The requirement is 1.25% to 2.50%, away from the track.
- Platform elevation above sea level, and the vertical datum.
- As-built ramp length, gradient, and elevation change, and the surveyed point where the ramp meets grade. The mapped runs, 199.1 m and 213.1 m, are in the R14 notes.
- Whether the built ramp is at the 6 m maximum, measured from adjacent grade to top of rail.
- As-built retaining-wall count, lengths, and heights. The five walls, 120 m to 230 m and 2.5 m to 6 m, are the 2013 concept.
- The stair from grade to the north access of the west platform. It is required. It is not in the photographs saved here.
- A count of shelters, and a count of catenary poles, at this stop.
- Whether the storage track conceived in 2013 on the northwest side of the stop was built.
- An openly licensed aerial, and an openly licensed frame in which the glass pyramids and the stop are both identifiable.
