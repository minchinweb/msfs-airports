# B254 Muttart Conservatory — modeling brief

Edmonton. Four glass pyramids and a small central skylight, with the Muttart stop. 9626 96A Street. Designed by Peter Hemingway. Opened in September 1976. The complex was finished in 1980. The stop node used by the project is 53.53621, -113.48000.

This sits with P152, the Muttart stop. It is not an observatory. It is not a bridge. The code is B254. The four pyramids, the foyer skylight, and the support greenhouses are this one building. It has no R code and no T code.

Numbers below are cited. Where two sources disagree, both are given. A line in "Still missing" has no published figure in the sources checked. Nothing here was filled by adding one citation to another. A floor area was not turned into a base width. A height was not turned into a slope length.

## Building

**What it is.** The Muttart Conservatory, a botanical garden in Cloverdale, in the river valley, south of the North Saskatchewan River. Peter Hemingway designed it in the early 1970s. The Edmonton Journal of 20 October 2017 calls him a British architect. The same article says the glass-encased complex opened in September 1976, and that the public waited until 1980 for the entire project to be completed. CBC, in the 40-year note of 2016, also says it opened in September 1976. Wikipedia lists the year as 1976.

The address is published twice. Wikipedia, the Journal, the City's current page, and OpenStreetMap relation 17644228 give 9626 96A Street. Wikipedia and the relation add NW. The relation also tags postcode T6C 4L8. A City news release of 30 August 2016 gives 9626 96A Avenue. Street and Avenue are both published. They were not reconciled.

**The four pyramids.** Tropical, temperate, arid, and feature. The Journal uses feature for the fourth pyramid. The City uses feature. A student slide on the architectural analysis uses show. Feature and show are the two names for that pyramid.

The Journal says the conservatory is symmetrically designed, with the pyramids connected by a central court. The student slide says the temperate and tropical pyramids sit on one diagonal, and the arid and show pyramids sit on the other.

Edify, in the 2022 renovation piece, says "the southern Temperate pyramid" and "the west-side Feature pyramid." OpenStreetMap names four separate building parts. The names are the tags on those ways. They were not assigned from size. Comparing the centres of the tagged rings: tropical is the northern part, temperate is the southern part, arid is the eastern part, and feature is the western part. Edify's two placements and those tags agree. The student slide's diagonals were not redrawn from the tags.

**Larger pair.** Tropical and temperate. The Journal says they measure 660 square metres, with a height of 24 metres from floor to apex. The student slide says 660 square metres and 24 metres from the floor to the apex. Wikipedia says the two larger pyramids are 660 square metres (7,100 sq ft). Wikipedia does not state a height. Williams Engineering says two large pyramids of 660 square metres each. Williams does not state a height.

Guinness writes 24 m (78 ft 8 in) and calls 660 m² (7,104 sq ft) a footprint. Monumentous states 7,100 square feet and 79 feet for the temperate pyramid, and the same for the tropical pyramid. The Journal does not state feet. The 24 m, the 78 ft 8 in, and the 79 feet are all published. They were not averaged.

**Smaller pair.** Arid and feature. The Journal says they are smaller, measuring 381 square metres and 18 metres in height. The student slide says 381 square metres and 18 metres. Wikipedia says the two medium-sized pyramids are 410 square metres (4,400 sq ft). Wikipedia does not state a height. Williams says two smaller pyramids of 410 square metres each.

The City's arid-pyramid page says that pyramid is 4,200 square feet and 59 feet high. The page does not state metres. It does not give a figure for the feature pyramid. Guinness writes 18 m (59 ft) and 381 m² (4,100 sq ft) for the two smaller pyramids, and calls the area a footprint. Monumentous states 4,200 square feet and 59 feet for the arid pyramid.

59 feet is 18 metres. That conversion was not used to rewrite an area. The 381 m², the 410 m², the 4,100 sq ft, the 4,200 square feet, and the 4,400 sq ft are separate published figures.

No source checked says the base is square, or gives a side. The areas were not turned into a base width. Where a source says floor to apex, that is the height. It was not turned into a slope length.

**Fifth pyramid.** Wikipedia says a fifth minor skylight pyramid lights the central foyer. The student slide says a small skylight sits over the centre court. The same slide says the centre court atrium is 1,319 square metres. That area is the student slide. It was not found in the Journal, on the City page, in Wikipedia, or in the Williams note.

OpenStreetMap way 714340902 has no name. It is tagged glass and pyramidal, and the height tag is 4. Its centre, 53.5352114, -113.4772875, sits between the four named parts. The way was not renamed from Wikipedia's sentence. The height tag is not a published height of the skylight.

The 2009 entrance is a different object. RPK Architects says the new entrance includes a fifth pyramid of black granite. That work was completed in May 2009. RPK states a facility size of 233 m² for the project. Kawneer supplied a 2000 Skylight for the entrance canopy. That skylight is not the glass of the four display pyramids, and it is not the centre-court skylight.

**Glass.** Guinness calls the four pyramids glass-and-steel. Edify says the designs are uninsulated glass, and that the arid pyramid has painted girders. Edify also says the four pyramids have housed plants in over 1,000 square metres of triangulated spaces since 1976. That 1,000 square metres is Edify's figure. It was not split into the floor areas above.

The photographs show a square pane grid, light mullions, a louvered band under the apex, and reflective glass. A pane size was not read from them. A published colour of the glass was not found. The photographs show the pyramids rising out of grass berms, with a pale sill at the glass line and openings cut into the berm. A berm height was not read from them.

The Peter Hemingway fonds at the University of Calgary names the conservatory among his projects, and dates it 1976. A drawing from that fonds, with a base side or a glass module, was not found.

The OpenStreetMap parts are tagged `building:material=glass`. That is a mapper tag. It is not a second specification of the frame. The height tags read 24 on the tropical and temperate parts and 18 on the arid and feature parts. The roof:height tags read 20 and 14 on those pairs. The tags repeat the Journal's metres. They are not a survey. The 20 was not subtracted from the 24, and the 14 was not subtracted from the 18.

**Support houses.** The student slide says six support greenhouses, linked by a tunnel, and that a 3,700 square metre greenhouse complex completed the project in 1980. A brochure quoted on dialectrix states the same 3,700 square metres in 1980. Williams says a set of six supporting greenhouses. Edify says nine greenhouses. Six and nine are both published. The 3,700 square metres is that support complex. It is not a display pyramid.

The houses sit between the stop and the pyramids. The western ring of the mapped complex, way 1287216690, is the ring nearest the stop. The 25 April 2018 photograph, taken from the conservatory grounds, shows that range: a row of low pitched glass roofs on pale frames, behind a chain-link fence, with downtown beyond. The Valley Line was not open. The stop is not in that frame. The 21 May 2022 photograph is taken at the stop and looks toward the same glass roofs, through the trees between the platform and the pyramids. A bay count was not taken from either photograph. A height was not read from either one.

## Plan

The group is east of the stop. The mapped main entrance, node 1940805166, is at 53.5354051, -113.4769722, on the east side of the eastern ring. The Journal says accessible parking is north of the main entrance. The river, and downtown, are to the north. The 3 November 2010 photograph looks that way.

OpenStreetMap has both outlines. Relation 17644228, read 14 October 2026, is one multipolygon for the named complex, with four outer rings and no inner rings. None of those rings is tagged with a pyramid name. The four named footprints are separate `building:part` ways. They are not members of the relation.

The eastern ring, way 1287216689, contains the main entrance. Its perimeter is 411.1 m. The way is tagged `leisure=garden`. The western ring, way 1287216690, is tagged `building=greenhouse`. Its perimeter is 442.5 m. Two untagged outer rings, ways 322972875 and 322972340, have perimeters of 25.7 m and 32.6 m. They sit at the south side of the western ring. They are not display pyramids.

The centre of the whole complex, area-weighted from those four rings, is 53.5354324, -113.4783154. The western ring is the larger part of that outline, so this centre sits toward the greenhouse ring. The centre of the eastern ring is 53.535221, -113.4772726. Wikipedia's coordinate, 53.53522, -113.47650, is a different point. It was not used.

The named parts, with the name taken from the tag:

- Tropical, way 714340898, northern. Centre 53.5355282, -113.4772852. Perimeter 115.4 m.
- Temperate, way 714340901, southern. Centre 53.5348974, -113.4772917. Perimeter 115.4 m.
- Arid, way 714340899, eastern. Centre 53.5352093, -113.4767941. Perimeter 89.5 m.
- Feature, way 714340900, western. Centre 53.5352144, -113.4777726. Perimeter 91.2 m.

Each of those rings has unequal sides. The sides are `edge_m` in `geometry.json`. A single base side was not taken from them.

**Map distance from the stop.** The stop node is 53.53621, -113.48000. It is west of the outline.

The nearest point on the complex is 28.4 m from that node. That point is on the western ring, the greenhouse ring. The nearest point on a named pyramid is 171.8 m from that node. That pyramid is the feature pyramid, the western one of the four. The eastern ring has the same nearest distance, 171.8 m. The centre of the eastern ring is 211.4 m from the stop node. The centre of the whole complex is 141.1 m from the stop node.

Those are horizontal WGS84 distances. They are not a clearance, and they are not a height.

## What reads at 1,000–3,500 ft AGL

The four glass pyramids are the landmark. Two read taller than the other two. They sit on grass berms. The square grid reads as a light frame on the glass. Plants inside do not read. The centre skylight is small beside the four. Between the stop and the pyramids, the support houses are a low row of pitched glass roofs. They read as glass, not as a second set of pyramids. The stop is the low slab to the west of that row. It is not this building.

No openly licensed aerial was found. The 31 August 2009 photograph is an oblique of the four. The 3 November 2010 photograph is the pyramids in the valley, with downtown behind them. The Valley Line is not in that frame. The line opened in 2023.
