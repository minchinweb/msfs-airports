# R6 Quesnell Bridge — modeling brief

Edmonton. Girder bridge across the North Saskatchewan River. Opened on 19 November 1968 (Edmonton Journal, 20 November 1968, cited by Wikipedia). Structurae's chronology says opened to traffic on 19 November 1969. The public page does not show a source for that 1969 day. Both dates are published. They were not averaged. Centre used by the project: 53.507, -113.567. The VFR checkpoint Quesnell Bridge is VCQSN, N53° 30.25′ W113° 34.00′. Carries Whitemud Drive (Highway 2), eight lanes.

Wikipedia names the lanes 4 westbound and 4 eastbound. The 2008 PIEVC report names them 4 southbound and 4 northbound after the widening. Both wordings are published. On the map the river crossing runs south to north. The east carriageway is one-way northbound. Ways 22916673, 944470769, and 297284104 run from 53.504846, -113.56648 to 53.507672, -113.566836, south to north. The west carriageway is one-way southbound. Way 22916674 runs from 53.507669, -113.567023 to 53.504845, -113.566675, north to south. A live read of the Fox Drive ramp on 14 October 2026 tags `destination:street=Whitemud Drive North`. That tag is on the ramp onto the east carriageway.

South end is Brookside and Brander Gardens. North end is Quesnell Heights and Laurier Heights (Wikipedia).

This is not W85 Fox Drive / Campbell Bridge, centre 53.5032, -113.5610. That is the creek span beside this deck. It is not W87 Rainbow Valley, 53.483, -113.555. It is not R8 Groat Bridge, 53.537, -113.539. It is not H36 Henday / Whitemud west. Do not copy those span lengths, widths, or pier notes here. This is not a truss.

Numbers below are cited. Where two sources disagree, both are given. A blank in the "Still missing" list has no published figure in the sources checked. Nothing here was filled in by adding one citation to another. OpenStreetMap `width=14.6` is a mapper tag on the four-lane ways. OpenStreetMap `maxheight=7.5` is a mapper tag on the north approach ways. Neither is a deck elevation.

## Bridge

**Type.** Girder (Structurae; Wikipedia). The City's 2018 anniversary note calls the original a prestressed haunch type girder bridge. The 2008 PIEVC report and the 2008 IABSE paper call the superstructure parabolic, precast, pre-stressed, and post-tensioned girders with a cast-in-place deck. Those wordings are all published. The OpenStreetMap road ways have no `bridge:structure` tag.

The 18 May 2008 photograph looks along the deck. Two circular concrete pier shafts stand in the river. The 29 August 2015 photographs, taken from the river after the widening, show a wide soffit, steel cross-frames between the girders, and paired circular shafts. Depth was not read from any of these frames.

**Spans.** Six (PIEVC 2008; IABSE 2008; City 2018). PIEVC: a 50 m simple span, a three-span continuous structure 164.7 m long, and a two-span continuous structure of 53.3 m and 46.6 m. IABSE: a 50 m simple span, three equal continuous spans of 54.9 m, and a two-span continuous structure of 53.3 m and 46.6 m. 54.9 m three times is the 164.7 m. The papers do not say which span is at which bank. The lengths were not reordered here.

**Length.** PIEVC 2008 and IABSE 2008 state 315 m. Structurae states 319.8 m. Wikipedia's infobox states 319.8 m (1,049 ft) and cites Structurae. The City's 2018 note states approximately 333 m. The 315 m, the 319.8 m, and the 333 m are all published. They were not averaged.

The OpenStreetMap west centreline, way 22916674, is 315.6 m. The east through ways, 22916673 then 944470769 then 297284104, are 101.9 m, 71.9 m, and 142.1 m. That is 315.9 m if the three are placed end to end. The east path, way 25748617, is 316.3 m. Use those lines for the plan shape. The mapped lengths and the published lengths are different extents.

**Girders.** Originally ten lines of parabolic precast prestressed post-tensioned girders (PIEVC 2008; IABSE 2008). Each girder had nine or ten post-tensioning cable ducts. Between six and eight cables were stressed before erection, and the rest after the deck was placed (IABSE preview). Girder depth is not published. The number of girder lines after the widening is not published as a total. PIEVC says the rehabilitation added girders, and that pier-cap post-tensioning was to take an increase of two girders in each direction. The new girders were to be NU precast (PIEVC). Those sentences are the addition. They were not added to ten here to make a new count.

**Piers.** Five concrete piers (PIEVC 2008). Each pier has two concrete shafts, a post-tensioned concrete cap, and concrete spread footings set into bedrock (PIEVC). The 2015 photographs show paired circular shafts. A count was not taken from the photographs as a published figure. Pier height is not published. A footing plan size is not published. The south abutment uses steel H-piles. The north abutment uses cast-in-place concrete bell piles (PIEVC). Approach slabs are 3.2 m (PIEVC). Bearings are described as elastomeric bearing seats (PIEVC).

**Clearance above the river.** Not published. No navigation clearance is published. No elevation above sea level is published for the deck, the soffit, or the water. PIEVC notes Transport Canada under the Navigable Waters Protection Act for changes in water level that affect clearance. It does not give a clearance. Environment Canada gauge 05DF001 is at the Low Level Bridge and uses a local assumed datum. No source used here converts a Quesnell clearance from that gauge. PIEVC states Edmonton's elevation as 668 m. That is the city. It is not a deck elevation.

The north approach ways, 22916676 and 297284108, are tagged `maxheight=7.5`. No published source found states 7.5 m. The tag is on the approaches, not on the river-deck ways. It is not a deck elevation.

**Rehabilitation that shows.** In 1988 a three-layer waterproofing membrane was installed over the concrete deck, a new asphalt wearing surface was applied, and four new expansion joint systems were installed (City 2018).

The 2008–2011 project replaced the deck, strengthened and added girders, lengthened and strengthened the pier caps, and widened the deck (PIEVC 2008; CBC; Global News). PIEVC, written in March 2008, describes the then-current bridge as six lanes, three each way, with the original 4.5 m east sidewalk, and the finished design as eight lanes, four each way, an emergency shoulder lane in each direction, and a 4.2 m pedestrian walkway. The City's 2018 note says the last major rehabilitation was completed in 2010 and widened the deck to four lanes on each side, and that the shared-use path is 4.2 m. CBC says the bridge fully opened to all traffic on 26 September 2011, and that a multi-use trail was the last stage of a $161 million project. Global News, 12 November 2010, said three lanes each way plus the Fox Drive ramp westbound would open by the end of that month, with waterproofing, paving, and an east-side concrete barrier still to come in 2011. The 2010 completion, the September 2011 opening, the 4.2 m path, and the eight-lane finished deck are all published. The 18 May 2008 photograph is older than this widening. The 29 August 2015 photographs are after it.

The original design was five lanes: two southbound, three northbound, 3.0 m outside shoulders to the concrete barriers, 0.6 m inside shoulders to the centre median, and a 4.5 m sidewalk on the east side for pedestrians and a bridle path (PIEVC 2008; Wikipedia citing that report). The bridge widens to the south, where the Fox Drive ramp merges (PIEVC). That merge is on this river deck. It is not W85.

Construction began in June 1967 (Wikipedia, citing the Journal). The City 2018 note states the original price as just over $2.7 million. Wikipedia, citing the Journal of 13 December 1966, states the approved plan cost as $8.8 million. Both figures are published. They were not reconciled.

## Deck

**Elevation.** 2018 LiDAR, CGVD2013. DSM median along the west carriageway, way 22916674: **637.80 m** (p10 636.82 m, p90 638.81 m, 102 samples). Along the east through ways: 22916673 **637.31 m**, 944470769 **637.96 m**, 297284104 **638.81 m**. Along the Fox Drive ramp on the deck, way 134926900: **637.46 m**. Along the east path, way 25748617: **638.74 m**. Those east medians were not averaged. River surface in this clip: **618.44 m**, valid. West carriageway median 19.36 m above that river surface. DTM at the project centre (53.507, -113.567): **618.43 m**. That centre sits over the channel, so the DTM there is the water, not a bank. DSM is first-return; traffic and railings can add up to 1 m. Rise is DSM minus the river surface, not a published clear height. Subtract deck depth if a clearance is stated. No published geodetic deck elevation was found to set beside these figures.

**Thickness.** The original deck was cast-in-place concrete (PIEVC; IABSE). The 2008 project replaced the deck. PIEVC lists cast-in-place concrete, reinforcement, a wearing surface, and a waterproofing membrane for the new deck. No thickness is given for the concrete, the waterproofing, or the asphalt. The depth from the wearing surface to the soffit is not published. The 1988 membrane and asphalt are gone if the deck was replaced. The City does not restate a thickness after 2011.

**Width.** Wikipedia calls this the widest road bridge in Edmonton and states eight lanes. A published overall width in metres was not found. Original outside shoulders 3.0 m, inside shoulders 0.6 m (PIEVC). The finished design in PIEVC has an emergency shoulder lane in each direction. A finished shoulder width in metres is not published.

OpenStreetMap tags `width=14.6` on the four-lane ways and `width=4.8` on the Fox Drive ramp. No published source found states 14.6 m or 4.8 m. Those tags are not a published structural width. Lane width is missing.

The two road centrelines are about 12.9 m apart at the south end and about 12.4 m apart at the north end. Those are distances between mapped centrelines. They are not a published width. The path centreline lies east of the east carriageway. That offset is not the 4.2 m path width.

**Lanes.** Eight on the finished bridge, four each way, asphalt (City 2018; PIEVC finished design; Wikipedia; OpenStreetMap). Each road way is tagged 80 km/h and `oneway=yes`. The south part of the east carriageway, way 22916673, is tagged three lanes. The Fox Drive ramp on the deck is tagged one lane. The rest of the east carriageway and the whole west carriageway are tagged four lanes. A live read of the same road ways on 14 October 2026 also has `lit=yes`, `hgv=designated`, and `hazmat=designated`. The west deck way also has `shoulder=both`. The extract does not store those tags. The tags are not a deck elevation.

**Sidewalks.** One shared-use path on the east side. The City 2018 note states 4.2 m. PIEVC's finished design states a 4.2 m pedestrian walkway. The original sidewalk was 4.5 m (PIEVC; Wikipedia). OpenStreetMap way 25748617 is the cycleway on the bridge, 316.3 m, node order south to north, tagged `foot=designated`, `bicycle=designated`, `surface=paved`. A live read also has `segregated=no`. The east road ways are tagged `sidewalk:right=separate` and `sidewalk:left=no` on that read. The ways run south to north, so the right side is the east side. Width was not measured from the 2008 photograph.

**Crossfall.** Not published. Longitudinal grade on the span is not published.

## Still missing

- Deck elevation above sea level from a published source, and the vertical datum. Soffit elevation.
- Clearance from the roadway or the soffit down to the river, and a navigation clearance. The 668 m figure is the city. The `maxheight=7.5` tag is on the north approaches.
- Which bank holds the 50 m simple span, and the order of the 53.3 m and 46.6 m spans.
- Girder depth, at the haunch and at midspan. A published girder-line count after the widening.
- Pier heights, and the plan size of the spread footings, the pier caps, and the abutments.
- Deck thickness, and the depth from the wearing surface to the soffit.
- Lane width, finished shoulder width in metres, and a published overall width. What the OpenStreetMap `width=14.6` and `width=4.8` tags measure.
- Crossfall as a slope. Longitudinal grade on the spans.
- Where each Whitemud approach meets natural grade, and the length, gradient, and elevation change of each climb.
- A published statement that settles Structurae's 19 November 1969 opening against the Journal's 19 November 1968 opening.
- An openly licensed aerial. City 2019 Pictometry clips in `aerial/` are measuring reference only (licence not confirmed).
