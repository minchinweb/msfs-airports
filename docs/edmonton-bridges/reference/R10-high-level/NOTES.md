# R10 High Level Bridge — modeling brief

Edmonton. Steel truss, two decks, opened 1913. Centre used by the project: 53.531, -113.511. Municipal historic resource (City of Edmonton Bylaw 11114, 13 September 1995).

The lower deck is the road. The upper deck is the streetcar. Do not swap them.

Numbers below are cited. Where two sources disagree, both are given. A blank in the "Still missing" list has no published figure in the sources checked. Nothing here was filled in by subtracting one citation from another.

## Bridge

**Length.** 2,550 ft, which the Canadian Register states as 777.24 m. That is the figure in the municipal designation: HistoricPlaces.ca place 3031, the Alberta Register record 4664-0031, the City statement of significance, and the City Archives history page ("2,550 feet (over 777 metres)"). The project's "about 777 m" matches this.

Other published lengths, not used as the structural length:

- 2,478 ft, about 755 m. Edmonton City as Museum Project (Herzog, 2014); *Canadian Rail* no. 381 (July–August 1984), conclusion; Edmonton Radial Railway Society; several of the compiled notes on the Alberta Register site record HS 54254.
- 777 m (2,549 ft). Wikipedia infobox.
- 2,800 ft, and separately "800-metre long", in two of the newspaper pieces compiled into HS 54254. Those two are outliers.

The designation does not say whether 2,550 ft is end to end of steel, centre to centre of abutments, or along the rail. The 2,478 ft figure may be a different extent. Both are recorded. Model to 777.24 m unless a drawing settles the endpoints.

**Spans.** 28 spans (designation). The designation itemizes 18 of them:

- 3 centre Pratt truss spans, each 87.78 m (288 ft), on four reinforced concrete piers in the river.
- 7 Pratt truss spans, each 29.26 m (96 ft), on the south approach.
- 6 tower spans, each 14.33 m (47 ft), on steel legs, forming the south approach.
- 2 Warren truss spans, each 39.62 m (130 ft), on the north approach.

3 + 7 + 6 + 2 = 18. The other 10 spans are not given lengths. The itemized lengths sum to 633.38 m, short of 777.24 m by 143.86 m. That remainder is not assigned to spans here.

Wikipedia and the CSCE historic-site note call the longest span 88 m (289 ft) and 88 m. *Canadian Rail* says the main Pratt trusses are about 288 ft between centres of end bearings, and that of 13 shorter spans, 7 were 96½ ft and the rest 47 ft.

The 1914 general elevation (*Railway Age*, vol. 56, no. 22, pp. 1194–1195, reproduced on Wikimedia Commons) figures the spans differently. Readings below are from that scan; the type is small. North to south, as far as the labels could be read: 129 ft 9 in and 131 ft 9 in (the two north Warren spans), then three river spans labeled about 291 ft 10½ in, 293 ft 3 in, and 291 ft 10½ in, then south-approach spans alternating near 97 ft 8 in to 99 ft 10 in and tower spans near 49 ft 5 in. Those plate figures and the designation's 130 / 288 / 96 / 47 ft are the same spans described to different rounding, or centre-to-centre of piers versus length between bearings. They are not reconciled here.

HistoricBridges.org calls the main spans Baltimore deck trusses. The designation calls them Pratt. Baltimore is a subdivided Pratt; the plate shows subdivided panels. Use the designation's names, and the plate for the panel pattern.

**Truss depth.** 50 ft (15.24 m) on the 1914 elevation, the figured distance from the top chord to the bottom chord of a main span. The decks sit inside that depth. The designation's 20 ft between decks is not the truss depth. Steel also continues below the lower deck ("steel substructure below the lower deck" in the designation), so 50 ft is the chord depth on the plate, not a claim about every member under the roadway.

**Which deck.** Upper deck: rail, now the High Level Bridge Streetcar, one centre track. Originally three tracks: CPR in the middle, a streetcar track on each side (Wikipedia; Alberta Register HS 54254; the 1910 *Edmonton Bulletin* description quoted in *Canadian Rail*). Lower deck: the road, with a sidewalk on each side. City of Edmonton lifecycle report IIS00488 (13 April 2022) says the same thing in current terms: streetcar on the upper deck, two southbound traffic lanes and two sidewalks or shared-use paths on the lower deck.

**Piers.** Four reinforced concrete piers in the river (designation). There is no single as-built pier height:

- Construction caption in *Canadian Rail*, Provincial Archives of Alberta B3316: four main river piers, each about 125 ft high. The HS 54254 narrative says the same, "about 125 feet".
- Herzog / ECAMP: four river piers, nearly 100 ft high.
- *Edmonton Bulletin*, 1910, quoted in *Canadian Rail*: the piers "will be 138 feet high", from the July 1906 low water to the top of the superstructure, and the top of the rails 198.8 ft above that low water. That is a pre-construction description, not a measured as-built height. The same article said the traffic deck would be 40 ft below the tracks; the bridge was not built that way (see Deck).

The 1914 elevation figures one south-approach tower at 140 ft 0 in, from the ground line up to the deck, and figures the crown of the roadway at a river pier as 153.2 ft above the July 1906 water level. Those are plate readings, not a surveyed pier schedule. Supports on the plate are numbered from 4 at the south end through 29 at the north end.

Pier count also disagrees. HS 54254's architectural line says 32 concrete piers and pedestals. Its history, and a *Canadian Rail* caption (PAA B3331), say four river piers plus 62 smaller piers under the approach spans.

**Clearance above the river.** The designation measures one thing: the base of the rail deck is 156 ft (47.55 m) above the North Saskatchewan mean water level. That is the height to use for the upper deck.

The project's "about 46 m" is the City Archives "Sketching History" page, which says the bridge rises 46 m above the river. The same office's history page says 156 ft "(over 45 metres)". 156 ft is 47.55 m, not 46 m and not 45 m. Other published heights, different datums or different points:

- Wikipedia infobox, clearance below: 48 m (157 ft).
- HS 54254 narrative: upper deck about 152 ft above mean river level; also 157 ft, about 160 ft, and "nearly 200 ft" in the compiled notes.
- *Canadian Rail* conclusion: about 160 ft above water. Radial Railway Society: top deck about 49 m (160 ft).
- CSCE, and ECAMP: federal legislation required the crossing to be 150 ft above the river. That is the reason it is a high-level bridge, not a measured deck elevation.
- A *Canadian Rail* caption (PAA B3313) says the valley is about 160 ft deep and the river is 800 to 900 ft wide. Valley depth and channel width, not deck height.

The mean-water surface is not tied to a geodetic datum in any of these sources. Environment Canada gauge 05DF001 (North Saskatchewan at Edmonton) is at the Low Level Bridge and publishes a local assumed datum, not an elevation above sea level.

**Along-bridge slope.** The 1914 plate labels the main spans "grade level" for both the crown of the roadway and the CPR base of rail. Over the south tower spans it labels both at 0.74%. A caption of the completed roadway (PAA B3307, in *Canadian Rail*) says the south end is about 10.34 ft higher than the north end. Those two statements are both kept. They are not the same measurement: 0.74% is figured on the south approach spans, and 10.34 ft is a caption for the whole bridge.

## Approaches

The steel approaches are part of the 777.24 m bridge, not earth fills. The south approach is the seven Pratt spans and the six tower spans on steel legs. The north approach is the two Warren spans. Lengths are the per-span figures above. Ten spans are still unitemized, so a single "south approach length" is not stated.

Beyond the steel, the road and the streetcar continue to the streets. Those extensions were rebuilt, so the 1914 grades are not the present grades.

**North approach.** On the 1914 plate the north roadway is marked grade level. The 1910 *Bulletin* description said the Edmonton-side roadway would approach the bridge at about the present level. HS 54254's environment line says the north approach gradient was level. No length and no elevation change, in metres, is published for the north street approach.

OpenStreetMap, retrieved 2026-10-14, has two road ways that are not tagged as bridge and that join the north end of the lower deck. Horizontal lengths only:

- East branch, way 510090943, 97.2 m, from 53.533588, -113.508957 to the bridge at 53.532735, -113.509277. This is the east entry. Wikipedia: 109 Street enters the left lane; going south, left is east.
- West branch, way 500327669, 109.4 m, from 53.533827, -113.509532 to the bridge at 53.53286, -113.509833. This is the west entry, the 110 Street side.

The streetcar way 219620022 runs 136.6 m from 53.533976, -113.509209 to the north end of the upper deck at 53.532768, -113.509572. A 1914 photograph (Ernest Brown, on Commons) shows the streetcar climbing toward the north end and passing under a separate plate-girder overpass. A *Canadian Rail* caption (PAA PA 58/16) calls that climb "a small incline". No gradient or rise is given for it.

Where these ways meet natural grade is not known. The coordinates above are the ends of the mapped ways, not surveyed grade points. Elevation at those points is not in the extract.

**South approach.** HS 54254 says the approach gradient was 5% on the south side. The 1910 *Bulletin* said the Strathcona side would turn out and rise 25 or 30 ft on a 5% grade. The 1914 plate labels a grade of 5.45% at the south end, where the steel meets the avenue. The avenue name on the scan is not legible. No length is figured on that 5.45% ramp, so the rise is not known. 25 or 30 ft is the 1910 proposal, not a measured as-built rise.

The south road approach was rebuilt. *Canadian Rail*: in 1931, $108,000 was spent on a better entrance to the traffic deck, with provision for a later west entrance. HS 54254: a new south approach, an eastern arm, was completed in June 1931 by the Canadian Bridge Company; in 1971 the girders at both approaches were rearranged and the hairpin turns replaced. The environment line still says 5%, and it also says the approaches have been altered. The present gradient is not published.

OpenStreetMap way 1095758755 is the south road after the bridge tag ends: 146.4 m, two southbound lanes, from the bridge at 53.52608, -113.513026 to 53.524814, -113.512607, bending east. HS 54254 says that at the Strathcona side both lanes curve to the east, and at the Edmonton side the roadway splits, one lane each side. That matches the mapped flares. The 146.4 m is the horizontal length of the mapped way, not a surveyed climb, and the far end is not confirmed as the point where the road meets grade.

The streetcar way 1474789002 continues only 25.3 m south of the upper deck, from 53.526029, -113.51328 to 53.525802, -113.513295. That is not the whole south climb.

## Deck

**Elevation.** Upper deck, base of the rail deck: 47.55 m (156 ft) above mean river level (designation). Not above sea level. The mean-water elevation itself is missing, so an ASL figure is missing.

The 1914 plate reads 153.2 ft from the July 1906 water level up to the crown of the roadway at a river pier. July 1906 water is a low-water line drawn on the plate, also marked against an "extreme high water" line. It is not the designation's mean water, and 153.2 ft is not the 156 ft rail-deck figure.

No source states the traffic-deck elevation above mean water. The designation's 20 ft between decks and the 156 ft rail-deck figure are left as separate measurements.

**Separation of the two decks.** Designation: the two decks are 6.10 m (20 ft) one above the other. *Canadian Rail* caption of the roadway under construction (PAA B3335): the top of the roadway is 19 ft 2 in below the base of the rail. The 1910 proposal's 40 ft was not what was built.

**Thickness.** Missing for the present deck. The lower deck is reinforced concrete on steel beams (HS 54254). The original wearing surface was creosoted wood blocks, 14 in thick at the crown (PAA B3335 caption). The wood was later replaced; HS 54254 records a lower-deck replacement in 1971. Fourteen inches is the original wood at the crown, not the present concrete thickness.

**Width.** Each deck is 11.89 m (39 ft) wide (designation). HS 54254's architectural line: bottom deck, 23 ft roadway and 8 ft sidewalks. 23 + 8 + 8 = 39, so the original sidewalks were inside the 39 ft. A second width, 43 ft (Radial Railway Society: 13 m), is given for the bridge overall in *Canadian Rail* and in HS 54254. That is wider than one 39 ft deck. It is recorded as an overall width, not as a second deck width.

**Road.** Two lanes, one-way southbound, asphalt (OpenStreetMap ways 22915822 and 1095758755; Wikipedia; City lifecycle report). Since 19 November 1980 ("Project Uni"), High Level traffic is southbound and Walterdale is northbound (Wikipedia). At the north end, 109 Street enters the left lane and 110 Street enters the right lane (Wikipedia). Trucks are prohibited. Posted clearance is 3.2 m (Wikipedia; OpenStreetMap `maxheight` 3.2). Wikipedia calls the lane width substandard and does not give it. Lane width is missing. Speed on the bridge ways is 50 km/h (OpenStreetMap).

**Sidewalks.** One each side of the lower deck, on brackets at the ends of the roadway floor beams (*Canadian Rail*). Original width 8 ft each (HS 54254). In the 1995 rehabilitation a west-side pedestrian and cycle path was added; the HS 54254 compilation says the work added 1.5 m to the sidewalk width. Wikipedia, citing the *Edmonton Sun* (14 July 2016) and Global News (14 September 2016): after the suicide barriers (July 2016) the west shared-use path is 2.8 m and the east path is 2.3 m. Those 2016 widths are the current usable widths in that account. The news articles themselves were not retrieved.

Handrails run the length of the bridge on both the east and west sides (designation). The bridge is painted black.

**Upper deck.** Same 39 ft structural width. One streetcar track in the centre now. The outer streetcar tracks were removed (the last of them in March 1967, *Canadian Rail*). The present width of the track bed, the rail section, and the walkable width beside the streetcar are not published. The upper deck is open: the truss edge is the side of the deck, not a wall.

**Crossfall.** Missing. The original wood blocks were thicker at the crown, so the 1913 roadway had a crown, but no crossfall rate is given. No current crossfall was found.

**OSM plan lengths**, horizontal, from `geometry.json`, for scale against 777.24 m:

- Lower-deck road centreline, way 22915822: 739.3 m. Shorter than the bridge because the north end is two separate lane ways and the structural ends are not this one way.
- Upper-deck streetcar, way 463555601: 770.5 m.
- East sidewalk way 461598491: 774.7 m. West sidewalk way 113767072: 791.3 m.

## Still missing

- Deck elevation above sea level, and which vertical datum. Also the elevation of the mean-water surface used in the 156 ft figure.
- Traffic-deck height above the river, as a stated measurement.
- Present deck thickness, roadway and sidewalk.
- Present crossfall.
- Present lane width.
- A single as-built height for the river piers. The published figures disagree and are approximate.
- Present north and south approach gradients, lengths, and elevation changes. The 1914 and 1910 figures predate the 1931 and 1971 approach rebuilds.
- The point where each present approach meets natural grade, and the elevation there.
- Lengths for the 10 spans the designation does not itemize, and a reconciliation of the 1914 plate dimensions with the 288 ft spans.
- Present width of the upper-deck track bed beside the streetcar.
