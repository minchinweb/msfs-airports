# R8 Groat Bridge — modeling brief

Edmonton. Girder bridge across the North Saskatchewan River at the mouth of Groat Ravine. Opened in 1955. Structurae's chronology says completion on 19 November 1955. The public page does not show a source for that day. Centre used by the project: 53.537, -113.539. Carries Groat Road, four lanes, as two carriageways of two lanes.

The east carriageway is northbound. Way 22916458 runs from 53.535607, -113.539669 to 53.538001, -113.537884, south to north, so the one-way follows the node order northbound. The west carriageway is southbound. Way 191843040 runs from 53.538044, -113.538008 to 53.535654, -113.539794, north to south. The October 2023 deck photograph is signed NORTH Groat Road, with the path on the right. That right-hand side is east. The mapped east centreline bears 23.9 degrees east of north. That bearing is the two mapped nodes. It is a plan direction, and it is not a published skew.

South end is River Valley Mayfair. North end is River Valley Glenora (Wikipedia).

This is the river span. It is not Wellington Bridge. Wellington is 102 Avenue across Groat Ravine, project code Z125. It is not the Stony Plain Road span. The project's G118 is that old span, demolished in 2023. The finished replacement is T133. G116, G117, Z125, T133, and G119 are the ravine decks in the same scene. Their briefs are in `reference/G116-hawrelak/`, `reference/G117-102-avenue/`, `reference/Z125-wellington/`, `reference/T133-stony-plain/`, and `reference/G119-107-avenue/`. They are not drawn here.

Numbers below are cited. Where two sources disagree, both are given. A blank in the "Still missing" list has no published figure in the sources checked. Nothing here was filled in by adding one citation to another. OpenStreetMap `hgv=no` is a mapper tag on the live road ways. It is not a deck elevation. The extract does not store a `maxheight` or a `width` on the bridge ways.

## Bridge

**Type.** Structurae calls it a girder bridge. The OpenStreetMap outline, way 626069075, tags `bridge:structure=beam`. Graham's project page says the 2020 superstructure is haunched steel plate girders, with a cast-in-place deck. DIALOG names weathering steel for the girders.

The superstructure that came off was concrete. Graham's case study says the existing bridge had seven spans and six cast-in-place girder lines, and that the existing design included rocking piers and hinges in the girders. Three of those girder lines came off in the first phase, the west half. The hinges were in the girders that were demolished.

**Spans.** Seven spans (Graham project page and the case study). Wikipedia says the bridge "is composed of two spans that are side by side." Global News uses the same words for the river bridge. Sam El Mohtar, in the Edmonton Journal, said the bridge is two sections, and that the west side would be demolished and rebuilt while traffic used the east side. The two side-by-side spans are the two carriageways. The seven spans run along the bridge. Individual span lengths are not published. They were not made by dividing a total length by seven.

**Length.** Wikipedia's infobox states 315.5 m and, in the same cell, 1,035 feet. The article text does not cite a measurement for that length. Structurae states 315.5 m. The public page does not show a citation for it. The City Archives scope line for a 2005 photograph says built 1955, 315 metres. Graham's project page says the river bridge carries traffic 1,000 feet across the river valley. Graham's case study says a 330-metre-long bridge. The 315.5 m, the 315 m, the 1,000 feet, and the 330 m are all published. They were not averaged.

The OpenStreetMap road centrelines are straight lines. The east carriageway, way 22916458, is 291.5 m. The west carriageway, way 191843040, is 291.1 m. Use those lines for the plan shape. The mapped length and the published lengths are different extents. The outline way is a closed ring. Its perimeter is 627.4 m. That perimeter is not a span length.

**Girders.** The girders on the bridge now are haunched steel plate girders (Graham project page), weathering steel (DIALOG). Girder depth is not published. The haunch depth is not published. The number of new girder lines is not published. The six cast-in-place lines are the old bridge. The 2008, 2009, 2012, and 2015 photographs show that concrete superstructure. It was removed. The 27 September 2026 photograph is the elevation after the replacement. Depth was not read from any of these frames.

**Piers.** Concrete river piers. DIALOG says the design reuses the majority of the existing river piers. Graham's project page says the faces above the waterline had partial-depth repairs and crack injection, and the base of three of the piers received a new concrete collar, to change the mechanics of the bridge. The case study is the source for rocking piers, as the existing design the temporary works had to fit. A pier count is not published. Which three piers received collars is not published. The photographs show concrete shafts in the channel. A count was not taken from them. The 2008 and 2009 frames are older than the collars.

Graham's project page also says select components of the existing piers and abutments were removed, and new cast-in-place substructure elements were built. Abutment size is not published. Bearing types are described only as "multiple bearing types."

**Clearance above the river.** Not published. No navigation clearance is published. No elevation above sea level is published for the deck, the soffit, or the water. Environment Canada gauge 05DF001 is at the Low Level Bridge and uses a local assumed datum. No source used here converts a Groat clearance from that gauge. Alberta Transportation's vertical-clearance list does not name this bridge. That list is overhead clearance on highways. It is not a height above the river.

**The same contract, other bridges.** Graham's project page lists three bridges. The river bridge is this one. The other two are the Groat Road bridge over Victoria Park Road, and the Emily Murphy Park bridge over Groat Road, also called the Mayfair Bridge (Global News; the Journal, 7 March 2018). The Mayfair bridge is south of the river. It is the project's G116, Hawrelak access. Their lengths and spans are not copied here. The City's demolition note places River Valley Road, and a shared-use path, under the north end of this river bridge.

**Rehabilitation that shows.** Last rehabilitated in 1989–90, before this project (Journal, 7 March 2018). Wikipedia says 1990, and cites the Journal article of 20 April 2018. What the 1989–90 work changed on the silhouette is not described.

The 2018–2020 work replaced the superstructure and kept the piers, with the repairs and the three collars above. Work on the road started 23 April 2018 (Global News; the Journal). Graham's contract completion date is October 2020. DIALOG's note of 29 October 2020 says the road was open to two lanes in each direction. The Journal of 29 October 2020 says all four lanes would reopen Sunday, and the photograph caption in that article says they were slated to open Monday. Both wordings are in that article. The east shared-use path went from 2.4 m to 4.2 m (Journal, 7 March 2018; Graham's case study). That is a wider deck edge on the east side. During construction the sidewalk was kept at 1.7 m, and cyclists were asked to dismount (Journal, 7 March 2018). That 1.7 m is the temporary walk. The City's project cost is stated as $48 million (Journal; Global News; Wikipedia). Graham states the contract value as $45.6 million. Both figures are published.

## Approaches

No source gives the present gradient, the elevation change, or the point where either road meets natural grade. The road was resurfaced from 87 Avenue to north of the river, and it was not realigned (Journal, 7 March 2018). The Journal of 29 October 2020 limits that road renewal to between 87 Avenue and the North Saskatchewan River. The City dropped a plan to reconfigure the Groat Road–Victoria Park interchange (Journal, 7 March 2018). Global News calls the construction zone about two kilometres, from the top of the south riverbank to north of the main bridge, and names traffic control from 87 Avenue to Victoria Park Road. Those are work limits. They are not a profile of the climb.

The OpenStreetMap ways below are the polylines stored in the extract. A far node is not a surveyed grade point. Lengths are horizontal WGS84. They are plan geometry only.

**South, Mayfair.** East carriageway, northbound onto the deck. Way 944417265 is Groat Road NW, two lanes, asphalt, 60 km/h, one-way, 47.8 m. Node order runs toward the bridge, from 53.535204, -113.539911 to the south end of way 22916458.

West carriageway, southbound off the deck. Way 141176415 is Groat Road NW, two lanes, asphalt, 60 km/h, one-way, `sidewalk=no`, 42.4 m. Node order runs away from the bridge, from the south end of way 191843040 to 53.535298, -113.540019.

Way 648645164 continues that southbound road for 918.2 m and passes within 10.2 m of G116. It was dropped. Way 106605160 is a one-lane link, destination Parks Area, 213.9 m, from the same south node toward the parks and G116. It was dropped. Neither is the climb to grade on this bridge.

**North, Glenora.** East carriageway, northbound off the deck. Way 25601763 is Groat Road NW, two lanes, asphalt, 60 km/h, one-way, `bicycle=no`, `foot=no`, 23.1 m. West carriageway, southbound onto the deck. Way 25601745 is Groat Road NW, tagged one lane, 188.2 m, from 53.539792, -113.537966 to 53.538212, -113.537877. Node order runs toward the bridge. Way 1098987150 is a one-lane link, 36.2 m, joining at that same node. Way 944414123 is the last 20.6 m, two lanes, one-way, onto the north end of way 191843040. The one-lane tag and the two-lane tag are both on the map. No published source states a lane drop there.

River Valley Road NW passes the north end, under the bridge. Ways 22914738 (33.3 m) and 141709487 (88.7 m) are tagged layer 1. Ways 933678761 (136.9 m) and 933678762 (168.8 m) are the same road in the extract. They are the road under the north end. They are not the climb. Victoria Park Road NW, way 157328051, is 85.2 m, two lanes, one-way, leaving the north end of the northbound carriageway. It is a cross street.

## Deck

**Elevation.** Missing above sea level, and the vertical datum is missing. Soffit elevation is missing. Height above the river is missing.

**Thickness.** The deck cast in 2020 is cast-in-place concrete, with hot-applied waterproofing, an asphalt wearing surface, and an HPC shared-use path (Graham project page). DIALOG lists high-performance concrete among the materials, with stainless steel rebar and weathering steel for the girders. No thickness is given for the concrete, the waterproofing, the asphalt, or the path. The depth from the wearing surface to the soffit is not published. The stainless steel rebar is inside the concrete.

**Width.** The shared-use path on the east side is 4.2 m. It was 2.4 m (Journal, 7 March 2018; Graham's case study; Wikipedia, citing that Journal article). The City's demolition note says the same 4.2 m path on the east side. Global News says the finished deck is wider, and says the sidewalks, plural, become shared-use paths. A west-side width is not in that article. Carriageway width is not published. Overall width is not published. Lane width is not published.

The two road centrelines are 9.8 m apart at the south end and 9.5 m apart at the north end. Those are distances between mapped centrelines. They are not a published width. The cycleway centreline lies east of the east carriageway, 3.3 m at the south end and 4.3 m at the north end. That offset is not the 4.2 m path width. The outline's north-end edge is 22.1 m. That is the mapped ring. It is not a published structural width.

**Lanes.** Four on the bridge. Two lanes each way, asphalt (Graham; the Journal; OpenStreetMap). Each road way is tagged 60 km/h and `oneway=yes`. A live read of the same two road ways on 14 October 2026 also has `expressway=yes`, `hgv=no`, `horse=no`, `lit=yes`, and `name:etymology:wikidata=Q139499492`. The outline also has `wikidata=Q5609886` and `wikipedia=en:Groat Bridge`. The extract does not store those tags. No published source found states a clearance that matches `hgv=no`. The tag is not a deck elevation.

**Sidewalks.** One shared-use path on the east side, 4.2 m, outside the northbound lanes. OpenStreetMap way 27265608 is the cycleway on the bridge, 291.1 m, node order south to north, tagged `foot=yes`, `bicycle=yes`, `surface=asphalt`, note "Open". A live read also has `segregated=no`. The east road way is tagged `foot=no`. The October 2023 photograph shows the path on the east side of the northbound lanes, with a concrete barrier on the traffic side and a metal railing on the outside. Width was not measured from that frame.

Graham calls the path HPC. The OpenStreetMap bridge way calls the surface asphalt. Both are published. The ways off the bridge, 1536910301 to the south and 27265610 to the north, are tagged concrete. They are the path leaving the deck.

A west sidewalk width after 2020 is not published. Global News uses the plural "sidewalks" and "shared-use paths." The Journal, Graham, Wikipedia, and the City demolition note describe the east path.

**Crossfall.** Not published. Longitudinal grade on the span is not published.
