# R9 Dudley B. Menzies Bridge — sources

Photos in `photos\` are openly licensed. A file that was useful but not openly licensed is listed under "Not downloaded" with the URL only. Four of the saved files are 1,280 px Wikimedia thumbnails: the full-size originals were refused with HTTP 429.

## Measurements

| Source | What it supplies | Licence / access |

| [PCL project sheet, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20120205221327/http://www.pcl.com/projects/Archived/E30032/index.aspx) | Builder’s description. 530 m seven-span continuous box, plus a two-span slab-and-girder structure at the north abutment and a four-span slab structure at the south abutment. Top deck approximately 10 m, two tracks by direct fixation, catenary poles and anchors on the deck. Box approximately 4.5 m deep by 4.5 m wide. Segments 2.5 m long, epoxy joints, post-tensioned. Holes in the webs and bottom slab. Walkway hung below the box for the three main river spans. Two spiral ramps to the valley trails. First concrete segmental box girder in Western Canada. Engineer: The Engineers Collaborative. Owner: City of Edmonton. | PCL page, captured by the Internet Archive on 5 February 2012. Text used for the dimensions. No photograph saved from the page. |
| [Hibbard, Heaton, and Gagnon, "Segmental Bridge for LRT Trains in Edmonton" (1990)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/gmp7ylffjpl) | Abstract only: the bridge is 600 m long and is a precast concrete segmental box girder; the design was constrained by environmentally sensitive ground. | The paper was not retrieved. No citation is shown on the public page for the 600 m or the quantities. Photographs on the page were not downloaded. |

| [McLachlan, "Edmonton Light Rail Transit Experience"](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/Onlinepubs/conf/1995/cp8/cp8v1-006.pdf) | Seventh National Conference on Light Rail Transit. Precast segments. University station opened August 1992, platform 23 m below road level, because of the elevation of the track leaving the south end of the bridge. Figure labels: Corona km 9.9, Grandin km 10.7, north portal km 11.0, south portal km 11.7, University km 12.3. | Transportation Research Board conference paper. The kilometre labels are printed to 0.1 km. |

Also: first segmental post-tensioned bridge in Western Canada, and the longest spans of that type in North America at the time of construction. No span length. | City of Edmonton. |
