# R15 Dawson Bridge — sources

Photos in `photos\` are openly licensed (CC BY, CC BY-SA, or CC0). A file that was useful but not openly licensed, or that is CC BY-NC, is listed under "Not downloaded" with the URL only. Four of the saved files are 1,920 px Wikimedia thumbnails: the full-size originals were not fetched. `east-portal.jpg` is the full file. The original is 1,745 px wide.

Photographs taken before the January 2010 closure show the truss that is still there and the sidewalks and deck that were replaced. The table says which ones those are.

| [Lima, DiBattista, and Kanji, TAC 2011 (PDF)](https://www.tac-atc.ca/wp-content/uploads/lima.pdf) | Five simply supported riveted through-truss spans, 43.3 m, 43.3 m, 43.3 m, 76.2 m, and 30.5 m from west to east. Length 236.5 m. Clear width 8.1 m. Train on the south lane, south truss about 10 percent heavier. Four concrete piers on spread footings, abutments at the banks. Deck history: 1912 timber, 1926 timber and asphalt, 1942 concrete on creosote planks, 1986 concrete 165 mm on 50 mm wood, 65 mm over the floor beams, pot bearings. 2010 sandwich plate 10 mm steel, 25 mm elastomer, 10 mm steel, 45 mm total. Panels typically 1.90 m by 8.6 m. Five lines of W460×74 stringers. Steel deck planar, no crossfall. Asphalt 40 mm at the edges to 100 mm at the centreline. Sidewalks rebuilt in wood, 2.65 m clear, from 1.5 m. Grade line not raised. Closed 4 January 2010, reopened 20 December 2010. Figure 1 is the City's general arrangement. | Transportation Association of Canada paper. Text used. The drawing in the PDF is too small to transcribe pier or water elevations. |

Length between abutment walls 236.5 m, and, in the project narrative, total length 236.6 m. Clear width 8.1 m. East span called the east approach span. The 76.2 m span called the navigation span. Opened 8 October 1912. Cost $145,000. Mine closed 1944. Face plates called 350AT. Panels typically 1.9 m by 8.5 m. Asphalt 100 mm at the crown and 40 mm at the shoulders. New members built to the original dimensions, lattice pattern cut into the web. Original sidewalks 1.5 m, new sidewalks 2.65 m. Rivets 19 mm or 22 mm. About 40 percent of the main members strengthened or replaced. The same general arrangement is reproduced, at 1 inch = 40 feet, with the spans labelled 142 ft, 142 ft, 142 ft, 250 ft, and 100 ft. | Canadian Consulting Engineer awards PDF. Text used.
