# V257 Edmonton River Queen — modeling brief

Edmonton. A steel passenger paddlewheel boat on the North Saskatchewan River. This is a vessel. The code is V257. It is not an R code.

The hull at the dock in 2026 is official number 817706. Transport Canada lists the name EDMONTON QUEEN (THE), with no former name, and lists the owner as Edmonton Queen Riverboat Inc. at 9734 98 Avenue NW. The registry page is marked modified 1 March 2019. The certificate on that page runs to 31 December 2029. The operator's site uses Edmonton Queen and Edmonton Riverboat for the same boat. Wikipedia says the operating name was Edmonton Queen from 1995 to 2018, and Edmonton Riverboat from 2018. The Journal, 6 May 2024, calls it the Edmonton Queen Riverboat. The 2016 auction sheet calls it the Edmonton River Queen. Those names are this hull. It was not replaced.

The model state is docked on the south bank, between the Low Level Bridge and the Tawatinâ Bridge. It is not underway. When it sails, the Journal says the route runs between the Dawson Bridge and the High Level Bridge and may change with the water.

Numbers below are cited. Where two sources disagree, both are given. A blank in the "Still missing" list has no published figure in the sources checked. Nothing here was filled in by adding one citation to another. A length read off a photograph was not used. OpenStreetMap coordinates are mapped. They are not a survey.

## Hull

**Which boat.** One hull. Transport Canada: year built 1993, registered 9 August 1995, port of registry Edmonton, IMO 8888886, construction carvel/flush, material steel, passenger vessel, paddle propulsion. The 2016 auction sheet says the keel was laid in 1992, construction was started by Scott Steel and completed by The Riverboat Corporation in 1995, and the boat was commissioned on 26 July 1996. Wikipedia says laid down 1992, completed 1995, and in service 26 July 1996. The Journal says the inaugural voyage was 4 May 1995. The 1993, the 4 May 1995, and the 26 July 1996 are all published. They were not collapsed into one year.

Transport Canada names the builder Scott Steel Ltd., city Edmonton. Wikipedia and the auction sheet say Scott Steel, Sherwood Park. The Journal says Scott Steel in east Edmonton. Both places are published.

The boat was sold at auction in 2016. CBC, 15 June 2018, says it then sailed as the Edmonton Riverboat after new flooring, bars, washrooms, and decor. In spring 2020 the hull was punctured at the dock. CBC, 17 July 2022, quotes the owner: wood pilings, and the boat sank. Wikipedia and CBC, 21 March 2024, say an underwater concrete piling. Wood and concrete are both published. CBC says it sailed again that Saturday in July 2022. No source describes a new hull outline. The Journal, 6 May 2024, says Eric Warnke, Megan Karius, Rob Davy, and Brittany Anderson completed a purchase that day, with sailings from 1 June 2024. The operator's FAQ, read on 14 October 2026, still describes this boat at the dock, including a wheelchair lift added as of 2026. Winter events on that site are dockside.

**Length.** Transport Canada states 51.78 m. The operator's FAQ of 16 June 2018 states 51.79 m (169 ft 11 in). The auction sheet states 169.91 ft. The Journal, 6 May 2024, states 51 metres. Wikipedia's article text states 52 metres (170 ft 7 in) and cites Maclean's, 30 April 2020. That Maclean's page was not opened. CBC, 21 March 2024, states 52 metres. The caption on the 2 January 2010 photograph states 170 feet. These were not averaged.

**Beam.** Transport Canada states 12.00 m. The 16 June 2018 FAQ states 12.01 m (39 ft 5 in). The auction sheet states 39.4 ft. These were not averaged.

**Draft.** The 16 June 2018 FAQ states 0.73 m (2 ft 5 in). The auction sheet states 2.4 ft, with a full crew of 12 and approximately 375 passengers. The same sheet lists passenger capacity as 399. Transport Canada states a vessel depth of 2.10 m. It does not state a draft. The 2.10 m was not subtracted from anything.

**Tonnage.** Transport Canada states 611.00 gross tons and 205.00 net tons. The 16 June 2018 FAQ and the auction sheet state 750.49 GT. The Journal states 750 gross tonnage. The 611.00 and the 750.49 were not averaged.

**Paddlewheels.** Stern wheels. Two of them. The 16 June 2018 FAQ states two paddlewheels at 18 rpm. The Journal states two 16-foot diameter wooden and steel paddle wheels. The auction sheet states paddlewheels at 18 rpm. Neither page says stern or side. The 12 June 2012 photograph shows two red wheels side by side at the stern. The 28 September 2008 side elevation shows the wheel at the stern. It does not show a wheel on the side of the hull. The 16-foot figure was not converted to metres here. No diameter was read from a photograph.

Transport Canada states two diesel engines, 700 brake horsepower, and 10.0 knots. The 16 June 2018 FAQ states two 350 bhp Cummins NTA855 engines, a 192 hp Volvo Penta bow thruster, and 9 knots. The auction sheet states 9 knots. The Journal states 10 knots. The 9 knots and the 10 knots were not averaged. The 700 and the two 350 bhp figures were not added together.

**Decks.** Two. The operator's FAQ, read on 14 October 2026, describes a top deck and a lower deck, with the dining room on the lower deck. The food page describes a bar in the dining room and a bar on the upper deck. The side-elevation photographs show an enclosed lower deck and an open upper deck under a blue canopy. The wheelhouse sits at the forward end of the upper deck. That wheelhouse was not counted as a third deck. CBC, 21 March 2024, names a restaurant, a wet bar, an open-air dining deck, and a dance floor.

**Colour.** No text source states a paint colour. The 2008, 2010, 2012, and 2018 photographs show a white house, a blue hull, a red stripe along the deck line, a blue canopy, and red wheels.

**Capacity.** The 16 June 2018 FAQ states 399 passengers and a crew of 10. The Journal states a rating of 399, and the photograph caption on that article says about 400. CBC, 21 March 2024, says more than 300 passengers. The 2 January 2010 photograph caption says the boat seats 250. These were not averaged.

## Heights

Freeboard is not published. Deck heights are not published. The height of the wheelhouse above the water is not published. The height of the canopy is not published. A stack height is not published. The side-elevation photographs do not show a tall stack above the wheelhouse or the canopy.

The Valley Line assessment's 10 m navigation window is recorded in the Tawatinâ brief. It was set off the Edmonton Queen. It is not an air draft of this boat. It was not brought into this file as a height.

The registry depth of 2.10 m and the drafts of 0.73 m and 2.4 ft were not turned into a freeboard.

## Dock

The City's September 2017 recreation assessment calls the south-shore access Rafters Landing, under Henrietta Muir Edwards/Rafters Landing. It lists a non-motorized boat launch and walking and cycling trails. It gives no dock length. The City's boating page does not list this berth among the City docks.

The operator uses the same place for the boat. The site footer says Rafter's Landing, 9734 98 Avenue NW. The FAQ says Rafters' Landing and Rafters Landing. It says the walk from the parking lot to the boat is approximately 200 metres north, and that four flags above the trees mark the boat. Henrietta Muir Edwards Park is just east, near the LRT bridge. OpenStreetMap names way 67563569 Rafter's Landing (Edmonton Riverboat). The spellings are Rafters Landing, Rafter's Landing, and Rafters' Landing. They were not edited into one form.

The mapped centre of that pier is 53.5395342, -113.4818826. That is the mean of its five corners. It is a mapped point. It is not a surveyed dock.

The pier way is a closed outline. Its perimeter is 29.6 m. The long edges are 12.5 m and 12.8 m. The short edges are 2.2 m, 1.1 m, and 1.0 m. Those are mapped edges. No published length or width of the dock was found. The mapped edges were not used as one.

Way 1373511013 is named Edmonton Riverboat and is tagged as a building and a restaurant. A mapper checked it on 5 August 2026. It is the mapped outline of the vessel. It is not a surveyed hull, and it is not an outline drawn for this file. The perimeter is 119.4 m. The longest span between its corners is 53.5 m. That span is not the 51.78 m registered length, and it is not the 51.79 m FAQ length. They were not reconciled. Two dirt paths in the same extract are named Edmonton Queen. They are trails. They are not in `geometry.json`.

CBC, 21 March 2024, says the sale included the gangway and the piles. The 2026 FAQ says the gangway is an incline up and then an incline down. It gives no length. The 28 September 2008 side elevation shows a blue covered ramp at the stern, up the bank. Another frame from that hour shows a stair at the bow. It was not saved. The 12 June 2012 photograph shows an open steel ramp beside the stern. The 2 January 2010 photograph shows white posts beside the stern wheel. No pile size was published. The wood and the concrete, above, were not reconciled.

**Bow.** The 18 June 2018 photograph was taken from the north side, at the usual mooring. The stern wheel is on the left of that frame and the bow is on the right. Looking south, the left is east. The bow points west. The 28 September 2008 frame shows the same side, and a through-truss beyond the bow. That truss is the Low Level. It is the through-truss west of this dock. The Tawatinâ was not built in 2008. The bow in these photographs points west, toward the Low Level. That is not a surveyed heading.

The longer diagonal of the mapped vessel polygon runs from 53.5395330, -113.4823342 to 53.5397415, -113.4816061. The bearing from the southwest end to the northeast end is 64.3°. The southwest end is the end toward the Low Level. The bearing is the mapped outline.

**Plan position.** From the mapped pier centre to the Low Level centre used by the project, 53.538, -113.487, the horizontal distance is 379.2 m. To the Tawatinâ centre, 53.5401, -113.4772, it is 316.1 m. The two bridge centres are 689.2 m apart. These are map distances. They are not distances along the river.

## What reads at 1,000–3,500 ft AGL

Include the docked boat in the Low Level / Tawatinâ scene. It is the white hull on the south bank in the gap between those bridges. At 1,000 ft the hull, the blue canopy, and the red stern wheels read against the bank. At 3,500 ft the boat is small beside the Low Level truss and the Tawatinâ pylons. It is still the hull in that gap. A model of the two bridges with an empty south bank there leaves the vessel out.

No openly licensed photograph was found that shows this boat with both bridges. The Tawatinâ path opened on 12 December 2021. The saved views are earlier, or they show one bridge.
