# H50 Henday / Yellowhead east: modeling brief

## 1. What it is

Anthony Henday Drive (Alberta Highway 216) meets Yellowhead Trail (Highway 16) east of the North Saskatchewan River, on the Strathcona County side of the city line. Centre used by the project: 53.568, -113.345. This is exit 54 on the Henday. Wikipedia names the Henday exit as "Highway 16 exit 400" for Yellowhead Trail.

Year: opened with the Henday northeast leg on 1 October 2016 (Wikipedia, Anthony Henday Drive, history section, citing the 2016 opening). A Wikimedia Commons caption says "Construction was completed in 2016." The contract was signed in May 2012 and the sod turning was on 16 July 2012 (same Wikipedia section).

Roads: Anthony Henday Drive north and south, both carriageways, three lanes each way on the mapped ways. Yellowhead Trail east and west through the junction, with the Yellowhead mapped as one-way ways on each side. Traffic runs east-west on Yellowhead and north-south on the Henday.

Not this structure:

- H52 Sherwood Park Cloverleaf, 53.513, -113.344, is a separate interchange about 6 km south.
- Y76 Yellowhead at Broadmoor, 53.570, -113.333, sits just east of this junction. Its Broadmoor and Lloydminster ramps are Y76 ways and are not copied here.
- Y75 Yellowhead / 17 Street, 53.5700, -113.3631, and the Bridges checkpoint (R20, R21, R22) are west.
- Y79 Bremner Cloverleaf, 53.5702, -113.2461, is further east.
- H49 130 Avenue, 53.5873, -113.3453, is north on the Henday.
- The Strathcona County rail overpass at Range Road 231 (opened 2023) is not this junction. It is north of Highway 16 on a different road.

The brief describes a railway through the middle, third-level flyovers, and braided ramps. The Wikipedia wording below supports the rail and the braided ramps. The rail itself is not located in any source checked.

## 2. Bridge

**Type.** Not published. No source checked gives the superstructure type, the girder type, or the material of the flyovers. The Commons photo `h50-henday-yellowhead-interchange-edit1.jpg` shows concrete columns and bent caps, with a steel-looking underside on the upper deck. No source confirms that material.

**Structures.** The junction is described as one interchange with several flyover levels. Wikipedia says the junction "includes several braided ramps" and is "bisected by a railway line". It says the rebuild has "two new semi-directional flyovers and one loop ramp for eastbound-to-northbound traffic". A Commons caption calls the design "a hybrid design with braided ramps and high-capacity third-level flyovers" (Wikimedia photo of the interchange). The Wikipedia article does not name this junction among its own "three-level interchanges".

**Span count and lengths.** Not published. No source checked gives a span count, a span length, or a structure length for any of the H50 flyovers or the Henday crossing.

**Piers.** Not published. The Commons photo shows single columns under some ramps and multi-column bents under others. No pier count or pier height is given in any source checked.

**Clearance.** Not published. The rail clearance, the road clearance under the Henday, and the clearance under the flyovers are all absent.

**Railway crossed.** The railway is a line that "bisects" the interchange (Wikipedia, citing Google Maps). No operator, track count, or crossing angle is given. Ask the rail operator or the City before placing the track. The extract in `geometry.json` has no railway way near the centre.

**Sidewalks.** Not published.

**Widening or rehabilitation.** The rebuild was part of the Henday northeast leg, opened 1 October 2016 (Wikipedia). Wikipedia gives 47 bridge structures for the whole northeast project. The project also had nine interchanges, two road flyovers, and eight rail flyovers. It does not say which of those belong to H50. Thirteen existing bridges were demolished across the project. The H50 share of those is not stated.

**OpenStreetMap bridge tags.** The OSM extract shows the Henday carriageways at layer 1 over the Yellowhead, and ramps at layer 1 and layer 2. The highest OSM layer here is 2. OSM does not show a third level. The third-level flyovers in the brief come from the Commons caption, not from OSM.

## 3. Approaches

**Henday south.** Anthony Henday Drive, three lanes, mapped 100 km/h. Gradient not published. Elevation change not published. The mapped south approach way is 409.0 m and 473.7 m (two ways). Its far node is at the edge of the extract. It is not the climb to grade.

**Henday north.** Anthony Henday Drive, three lanes, mapped 100 km/h. Gradient not published. Two mapped approach ways, 166.4 m and 641.1 m. The 641.1 m way starts about 0.9 km north. It is not the climb to grade.

**Yellowhead west.** Yellowhead Trail, two lanes each way in the extract, toward the Bridges checkpoint. Gradient not published. Where it meets natural grade is not published. The mapped way is 708.3 m, and it continues past this junction.

**Yellowhead east.** Yellowhead Trail east toward Broadmoor and Lloydminster. Gradient not published. This end belongs to Y76. Its mapped way starts at 53.569958, -113.341091.

**16 West and 216 North links.** Ramps mapped at 66.3 m to 1,070.3 m. These are plan lengths of OSM ways. No gradient is published for any of them.

If unpublished, the approach grade is unpublished. Nothing in this folder gives a grade.

**Elevation above road or rail.** Not published.

**Deck thickness.** Not published.

**Width.** Not published. The OSM tags are `width=11.1` on the Henday and `width=5` on one link. These are mapper tags, not published widths. They are not used here.

**Lanes.** Henday three lanes each way on the mapped ways (OSM). Wikipedia's lane-count entry gives "6 lanes" for the Highway 16 to Baseline Road segment as of April 2020. That is a segment count and the segment ends are not H50's.

**OSM maxheight.** No maxheight tag is in the H50 extract. Nothing here uses a maxheight tag.
