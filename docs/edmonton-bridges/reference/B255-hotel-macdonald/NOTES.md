# B255 Fairmont Hotel Macdonald — modeling brief

Edmonton. Château-style hotel on the north bank, at 10065 100 Street. The hotel's own history page calls it Fairmont Hotel Macdonald, and says the Grand Trunk Pacific Railway Company opened it on 5 July 1915. The City statement of significance calls the style Canadian château, names the architects Ross and MacFarlane, and places the building on 100 Street and McDougall Hill, overlooking the North Saskatchewan River valley. HistoricPlaces.ca uses the same architect and the same seven-storey description, and spells the hill MacDougall Hill.

The ask wrote Hotel MacDonald. That spelling is kept here as the ask's spelling. It is also an other name on HistoricPlaces.ca, an other name on the Alberta Register (Hotel MacDonald), and the OpenStreetMap `name` tag, Fairmont Hotel MacDonald. The City heading is MACDONALD HOTEL. The City statement says the building was named after Sir John A. Macdonald. HistoricPlaces.ca spells the prime minister Sir John A. MacDonald. The building is not renamed to MacDonald.

It belongs in the north-bank scene with the 100 Street funicular. The funicular notes place the upper end of the mapped track south of this hotel, at 100 Street. The Journal, 7 March 2016, places that work south of the Hotel Macdonald at 10065 100 St. This hotel is the building at the top of that bank. This is not the funicular. The funicular track is way 490235928. This is not the Grierson Hill Bridge. That span is way 106711211. This is not a Low Level deck. The 1900 northbound deck is way 31882107. The 1948/1949 southbound deck is way 22916473. It has no R code. The building code is B255.

Numbers below are cited. Where two sources disagree, both are given. A blank in the "Still missing" list has no published figure in the sources checked. Nothing here was filled in by adding one citation to another, or by turning a storey count into a height. OpenStreetMap `height`, `building:levels`, `building:material`, and `roof:colour` are mapper tags. They are not a published height, a storey count, a façade specification, or a roof specification. No elevation was stored.

## Massing

**Plan.** The designation calls the building L-shaped: perpendicular wings, a recessed diagonal entrance, and a turret (City statement of significance; HistoricPlaces.ca, source Bylaw 7700). The Alberta survey file HS 5265 codes the plan shape as irregular. The L-shaped description and the irregular code are both published. They were not reconciled into one outline.

A published plan length was not found. A published width was not found. The municipal designation does not give a footprint in metres. The OpenStreetMap ring is below, under Plan. It is a mapped perimeter. It is not that missing footprint.

**Wings.** Two perpendicular wings, in the designation's words, with the entrance on the diagonal. The City statement says the building fronts 100 Street and McDougall Hill, with Frank Oliver Memorial Park between the hotel and Jasper Avenue, and with the rear façades toward the river valley. HistoricPlaces.ca says MacDougall Hill in that same sentence. The entrance portico is on the street face: five arches, four pillars, and two pilasters (City statement; HistoricPlaces.ca). The river face is the rear. The 17 May 2013 photograph is captioned as the south face. The 7 August 2011 photograph is captioned as the view from the North Saskatchewan River. Neither frame was used to measure a wing.

**Storeys.** Three published counts. They were not averaged. A storey count is not a height.

The City statement, HistoricPlaces.ca, the City archives sketching page, and Historic Hotels of the World — Then and Now each say seven storeys. The designation's phrase is seven-storey Indiana limestone façades.

The survey file's compiled history says the original hotel was 10 storeys and had 175 rooms. That sentence sits in the same field as other dates that disagree with the opening day below.

Emporis, on the page archived 4 March 2016, states 11 floors above ground. Wikipedia states 11 floors and cites that Emporis page. The OpenStreetMap way is tagged `building:levels=1`. No published source found states 1 storey. The tag was not used.

**Roof.** Steeply sloped dormered roofs, high chimneys, projecting towers, a turret roof, and finials (City statement; HistoricPlaces.ca). The survey codes the roof structure as high gable, the dormer type as hip, and the towers as a tower, and it lists a cupola or lantern, a balustrade, and finials among the roof-trim codes. Copper roofing is a character-defining metal (City statement; HistoricPlaces.ca). The survey's exterior note says sheet copper roofing, and one compiled paragraph says the copper roof was blackened with age. The 8 October 2011 photograph shows green on the steep roof and on a conical turret. The 17 May 2013 night photograph shows the roofs dark. The blackened sentence, the green in the daylight frame, and the dark roofs in the night frame were not averaged. No height was read from either frame.

**Height.** The designation does not give a height. A height was not made by counting storeys.

Wikipedia states 47.7 m (156 ft) and cites Emporis. The Emporis page in the 4 March 2016 archive does not state 47.7 m and does not state 156 ft. It states an architectural height of 155.51 ft and a tip height of 167.49 ft. The 47.7 m and the 156 ft are Wikipedia's pair. The 155.51 ft and the 167.49 ft are Emporis's pair. They were not converted. They were not averaged. Emporis does not say that the architectural height is the ridge. It does not say that the tip is a turret.

The OpenStreetMap way is tagged `height=47`. The tag does not name a unit on the element. It was not used as the 47.7 m, and it was not matched to either Emporis figure.

Wikipedia's list of the tallest buildings in Edmonton puts 47.7 m (156 ft) and 11 floors on a row named Hotel Macdonald Annex, for the years 1915–1965. The annex was built in 1953 and demolished in 1986, on the dates below. The row name and the years were not reconciled. The row was not used as a height for the demolished wing, and it was not used as a second copy of the 47.7 m.

**The 1953 wing, which is gone.** It is not the present roof. The survey file says an addition opened on 3 January 1953, with 300 rooms, and it calls that addition a modern 13-storey wing and, in another sentence, a 16-storey addition of 1952–1953. Wikipedia calls it a 16-storey wing of 292 rooms, north of the original building, and says it was demolished in 1986. The same article also says the expansion wing was demolished in 1983. Historic Hotels of the World says a 16-storey, 300-bedroom addition, demolished in 1986. The City archives page says the 1953 addition was demolished in 1986. SkyscraperPage lists the MacDonald Hotel Annex at 10021 100th Street NW, finished 1953, destroyed 1986, 15 floors, and does not state a height. The 13, the 15, and the 16 were not averaged. The 292 rooms and the 300 rooms were not averaged. The 1983 sentence and the 1986 date were not averaged. No height of that wing was published in the sources checked, and none was used as the château height.

The hotel closed in 1983 (City statement; HistoricPlaces.ca). It reopened in May 1991 (City statement; HistoricPlaces.ca). Wikipedia and Historic Hotels of the World date the reopening 15 May 1991. Emporis lists a renovation in 1987. The 1987 renovation line and the May 1991 reopening were not combined.

**Opening day.** Fairmont and Wikipedia say 5 July 1915. The survey file also records 6 July 1915, 15 July 1915, a completion in July 1912, and a Journal excerpt dated 31 July 1913. Those dates are all in that file. They were not averaged. Construction is given as 1912 to 1915 on HistoricPlaces.ca, as 1911 to 1915 on the Emporis archive, and as started 1913 and ended 1915 in the survey's construction dates. A September 1912 start is also in the survey narrative. The start years were not averaged.

## Materials

**Walls.** Indiana limestone façades (City statement; HistoricPlaces.ca). The survey says a steel and reinforced-concrete skeleton under Indiana limestone and sheet copper, and says restoration stone was brought from near the original quarry. Emporis, in the 2016 archive, says the structural material is masonry, the façade material is sandstone, and the façade colour is brown. Wikidata states sandstone and does not cite a source on that statement. The OpenStreetMap way is tagged `building:material=sandstone` and `building:colour=#eee1ce`, and the way's `source` is Bing. The limestone, the sandstone, and the brown were not averaged. The designation's material is Indiana limestone. The sandstone statements are the conflicting figures.

**Roof metal.** Copper (City statement; HistoricPlaces.ca; the survey's exterior note). The colour conflict is under Massing. The OpenStreetMap way is tagged `roof:colour=#808000`. That tag was not read as a colour name.

**Entrance face.** The street face, not the river face. Five arches, four pillars, two pilasters, gargoyles, and the crests of the four western provinces (City statement; HistoricPlaces.ca). The 8 October 2011 photograph shows that portico, the pale stone, and the green roof and conical turret above it. No arch was counted from the frame.

## From the air

The hotel belongs in the Low Level north-bank scene. It is the building at the top of the bank the funicular climbs, and the north end of the Low Level is the river crossing below that bank.

At 1,000–3,500 ft AGL the part that matters is the roof and the turrets. The designation's silhouette is the steep dormered copper roof, the chimneys, the towers, and the turret. The 9 August 2012 photograph shows that roof as a low château in front of the flat-roofed towers. The 7 August 2011 photograph shows the same roof from the river, on the bank. The 8 August 2017 photograph shows the roof at the top of the bank, with the Low Level truss in the same frame. The pale stone is the wall under that roof. Interior rooms do not read at that height. No height was read from the frames. No storey was counted from them.
