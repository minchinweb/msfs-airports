# G117 102 Avenue at Groat Road — modeling brief

102 Avenue NW over Groat Road. First crossing north of Groat Bridge. Centre used by the project: 53.5430, -113.5403. Alberta file B408, 102 AVE - GROAT RD BRIDGE FLYOVER, year 2016.

## What it is

This is the 2016 flyover. It replaced a steel trestle.

It is not the Groat Road bridge over Victoria Park Road. Graham's contract worked that span in 2019. Victoria Park Road is at the north end of the river bridge. The Journal (7 March 2018 and 20 April 2018) says Groat Road bridge over Victoria Park Road. Global News on 20 April 2018 reverses the roads and says Victoria Park Road bridge over Groat Road. In the geometry extract, Victoria Park Road NW is a connected way of the river span, not a bridge way. No separate bridge object was found. No sixth folder was written.

This is not Wellington Bridge. Wellington is 102 Avenue across the ravine to the west, Z125. This is not the river span. Do not reuse the river span's lengths, the east path width, or the pier notes.

## Bridge

**Type.** Single-span steel girder with integral concrete abutments. The design paper, "The 102nd Avenue Bridge over Groat Road – Design Concept and Challenges," says a 113 m long bridge, an 83 m main steel span connected to 15.6 m long integral concrete abutments, hybrid steel and concrete piles at the back of each abutment, and sliding bearings at the front. The EIA plate caption calls the existing 102 Avenue bridge a single-span steel-haunched girder, a clear span, with steep vegetated abutment slopes. The photograph context is 2018, so that caption is this bridge. "Steel-haunched" is the EIA wording. The design abstract says a steel span and does not say haunched. Both wordings are kept.

ENR, 20 April 2015, says the span is roughly 100 meters, and the girders are roughly 4.2 meters deep at their ends. That 4.2 m is the girder depth at the ends of this bridge. It is not the river bridge's shared-use path. ENR also says seven central girder sections were being placed, and four girders buckled on 16 March 2015. A girder-line count was not taken from the seven sections. The initial budget in that article is $32 million.

Alberta's row for B408 prints GF 1 and year 2016. The 1 matches one main span. The row prints 83.0 beside the year, the same number as the 83 m main span. It also prints 83.3, 18.2, and 68.3. Those three were not assigned to a length or a height.

The 83 m, the 15.6 m, and the 113 m were not added together.

**What it replaced.** A bridge on steel trestles, described as 104 years old in the design abstract. The Journal and Global News, 14 July 2016, say the old bridge was built in 1910 and demolished in July 2014. The new bridge reopened at 10 a.m. on Friday 15 July 2016. The price named in those articles is $32 million. Traffic opened as one lane each way, then two lanes each way at peaks, with work continuing into the fall. The 9 September 2016 photographs still show cones.

**Ravine.** The design abstract says Groat Road is in a 22 m deep ravine. That is the depth of the ravine. It is not a deck elevation. It was not added to a gauge.

**Length.** Published figures, kept separate: 113 m (design paper); 83 m main span (design paper, and 83.0 on the Alberta row); 15.6 m abutment length (design paper); roughly 100 m (ENR). The mapped road centreline, way 25601853, is 111.9 m horizontally. Use it for the plan shape. It is not the 83 m main span.

## Deck

**Elevation.** Not published. No clearance over Groat Road was found in the abstract. Gauge 05DF001 was not converted.

**Width.** Not published. Way 25601853 tags width 13.6. No published source found states 13.6 m. The Journal and Global News say four lanes, wider sidewalks, and a bike lane. They do not give the widths.

**Lanes.** Four (Journal; Global News; way 25601853). Asphalt, 50 km/h. Node order is west to east. No oneway tag. A live read on 14 October 2026 also has hgv=no. That tag is not a clearance and not a deck elevation.

**Sidewalks and bike lane.** Way 114503872, footway, 120.9 m, north of the road centreline. Way 114503875, footway, 112.1 m, south of the road centreline. Way 1230468990, 102 Avenue NW Protected Bike Lane, asphalt, 111.7 m, between the road centreline and the north footway.

The 9 September 2016 photographs show the deck: lanes, a bike lane separated by a concrete barrier, a sidewalk, and a metal railing. Groat Road is not in those frames. The river span is not in those frames. Wellington Bridge is not in those frames. None of the three is a side elevation.
