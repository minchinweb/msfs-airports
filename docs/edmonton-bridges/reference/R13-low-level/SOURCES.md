# R13 Low Level Bridge — sources

Photos in `photos\` are openly licensed (CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0). A file that was useful but not openly licensed, or that is CC BY-NC, is listed under "Not downloaded" with the URL only. All eleven saved files are 1,920 px Wikimedia thumbnails. The full-size originals were not fetched.

Photographs taken before the December 2025 southbound repairs show the truss outline that is still there. The City's project page does not describe a new outline from that work. The 2027 northbound work, including the timber pedestrian decking named on that page, has not been done. Where James MacDonald's girder is in the frame, the table says so.

| [Lima, Robson, Oosterhof, Kanji, DiBattista, and Montgomery, CSCE 2008](https://studylib.net/doc/8911448/rehabilitation-of-a-100-year-old-steel-truss-bridge) | Northbound span only. Riveted steel through-truss, four equal spans of 53 m, built 1898–1900. Three concrete piers on timber piles, abutments at the banks. Members of angles, cover plates, gussets, and lattice. Grand Trunk Pacific loading, streetcar track offset. Streetcar removed around 1939. Twinned in 1948: original then northbound only, new span southbound. Sidewalks on one side of each bridge, none between. Northbound sidewalk on the east side. Railway abandoned circa 1950. 1980: truss centrelines widened from 6096 mm to 7925 mm by shifting the downstream trusses, new stringers and floor beams, new concrete deck. 2006: about 40 percent of the main members replaced in 350WT steel to the same cross-section, lattice plasma-cut, bolted connections, bridge repainted, sidewalk nearly twice as wide, top 50 mm of the deck replaced with a silica fume overlay. Appearance to be kept. 'A' register. | CSCE 2008 Annual Conference paper, Québec, 10–13 June 2008. Text read from the studylib copy. A publisher PDF was not found. Figures were not extracted into `photos\`. |
| [City of Edmonton, Low Level Bridge rehabilitation](https://www.edmonton.ca/projects_plans/roads/low-level-bridge-rehabilitation) | Two structures. Northbound built 1900, the oldest bridge in the city. Southbound completed in 1949 and designed to visually match it. Southbound work in 2025: scaffolding under each span, corrosion cleaning, steel members strengthened and replaced. Finished December 2025. Both spans back in their normal traffic pattern. Walking and cycling kept on the northbound sidewalk during that work. Northbound work anticipated in 2027: components replaced and strengthened, guardrail upgraded, timber pedestrian decking replaced. Shared pathway under the bridges kept open. | City of Edmonton. Text used. Page photos were not downloaded. |

The memo says the southbound span would be maintained rather than rehabilitated, for a period estimated at up to 10 years, and then decommissioned. The 2025 repairs are later than this memo. Northbound rehabilitation from 2027: painting, sidewalk repairs, components replaced and strengthened, so it can stay in service until a replacement exists. After that, vehicles leave both spans, the southbound span is demolished, and the northbound span is kept for active use or demolished. High Level Bridge is the one designated by Bylaw 11114. | City of Edmonton council file. |
