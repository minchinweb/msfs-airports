# R13 Low Level Bridge — modeling brief

Edmonton. Two riveted steel through-truss bridges side by side across the North Saskatchewan River. The downstream span was built between 1898 and 1900. It is the oldest river crossing in the city. The upstream span was added in 1948 and completed in 1949. Centre used by the project: 53.538, -113.487. Together they carry four lanes, from Rossdale Road and McDougall Hill Road on the north bank to Connors Road and Scona Road on the south bank.

This is not the James MacDonald Bridge. James MacDonald is the 1971 girder on 97/98 Avenue, six lanes, centre 53.536, -113.488. It stands upstream of this pair. Its deck, its piers, and its geometry are not in this file.

Both of these spans are still there. The City's May 2026 report says the southbound span is to be decommissioned after a replacement bridge is built. That has not happened. One span alone reads as a mistake.

Numbers below are cited. Where two sources disagree, both are given. A blank in the "Still missing" list has no published figure in the sources checked. Nothing here was filled in by adding one citation to another. OpenStreetMap `maxheight` tags are mapper tags. They are not deck elevations.

## Bridge

**Which span is which.** The City's northbound bridge was completed in 1900. The City's southbound bridge was completed in 1949. Wikipedia says a twin of the same design was added upstream in 1948. Commons lists the James MacDonald Bridge as the next crossing upstream, and the Tawatinâ Bridge as the next crossing downstream.

OpenStreetMap way 31882107 is the eastern centreline. Its nodes run from the south bank to the north bank, and it is tagged one-way, so the traffic is northbound. Way 22916473 is the western centreline. Its nodes run from the north bank to the south bank, so the traffic is southbound. The west deck is the side toward James MacDonald. That is the upstream side. The 1900 span is the downstream deck, northbound, way 31882107. The 1948/1949 span is the upstream deck, southbound, way 22916473.

Wikipedia also says the 1948 span has carried all east-bound traffic since the original span was widened, and that the original span then carried west-bound traffic. The City's words are northbound and southbound. Those are the words used below.

**Type.** Both are through trusses. The 2008 paper, written about the northbound span, calls it a riveted steel through-truss. HistoricBridges calls the structure a 7-panel rivet-connected Pratt through truss. Structurae, on the 1900 record, says through truss, Pratt type, steel. Wikipedia's infobox says through Pratt truss.

The southbound span was designed to visually match the original (City project page). The May 2024 memo says it was designed and built to resemble the northbound structure but is unique. Wikipedia says a twin span of the same design. HistoricBridges says very similar design details. The elevation photographs show two horizontal-chord through trusses side by side. A separate truss name for the southbound span is not published.

**Spans.** Northbound: four equal spans of 53 m (2008 paper). HistoricBridges states the main span as 170.0 feet (51.8 m), and four main spans, on the page whose structure length is 699.0 feet (213.1 m). That length is the northbound length. 53 m and 51.8 m are both published. They were not averaged. HistoricBridges states 7 panels. The 2008 paper does not give a panel count.

Southbound: Wikipedia's infobox says 4 spans. It does not give a separate count for each structure. No source found states the southbound span lengths one by one.

**Length.** Northbound: 213.1 m. HistoricBridges states 699.0 feet (213.1 m). Structurae states 213.1 m for the 1900 structure. Wikipedia's infobox states 213.1 m (699 ft) for the northbound span. The 2008 paper states four equal spans of 53 m and does not state a total. The CSCE historic-site page states a total length of 213 m for the four Pratt spans, and then says the bridge was twinned.

The OpenStreetMap northbound centreline, way 31882107, is 213.9 m. Use 213.1 m as the published structure length. Use the OpenStreetMap line for the plan shape. The mapped length and the structure length are different extents.

Southbound: Wikipedia's infobox states 211.7 m (695 ft). The article text does not cite a measurement for that length. No other source found states 211.7 m. The OpenStreetMap southbound centreline, way 22916473, is 212.6 m. The 211.7 m and the 212.6 m were not reconciled.

**Truss.** Northbound members are built up from structural steel angles, cover plates, gusset plates, and lattice (2008 paper). HistoricBridges names the builder of the 1900 bridge as the Dominion Bridge Company of Montréal, and also names the Winnipeg works. The CSCE historic-site page says the 1900 bridge was designed and built by engineers from the federal government, and that the 1948 twin used steel fabricated by the Dominion Bridge Company in Winnipeg. HistoricBridges says the 1949 bridge was also built by Dominion Bridge. Both accounts of the 1900 builder are published.

In 1980 the spacing of the northbound truss centrelines was widened from 6096 mm to 7925 mm. The downstream trusses were shifted further downstream, the piers and abutments were widened, and new stringers, floor beams, and a concrete deck were built (2008 paper). Downstream on this span is the east side, away from the upstream twin. The 7925 mm is the centreline spacing of the northbound trusses after 1980. It is not a clear roadway width. It is not the width of the pair.

Truss depth is not published for either span.

**Piers.** Northbound: three concrete piers on timber piles in the river, and an abutment at each bank (2008 paper). Wikipedia's infobox says 3 piers in the water. It does not split that count by span. Pier height is not published. A plan size of the piles or the footings is not published.

The photographs show a concrete shaft under each truss, wider toward the water. The September 2026 photograph shows two pier faces side by side, one under each floor system. A pier count and a foundation for the southbound span alone are not published.

**Clearance above the river.** Not published for either span. No navigation clearance is published. No elevation above sea level is published for the deck, the soffit, or the water.

Wikipedia, citing the Edmonton Bulletin of 30 June 1915, says the 1915 flood peaked just below the deck, and that the peak was estimated at about 13 m above low water. That 13 m is the flood above low water. It is not a clearance. It was not turned into a deck elevation here.

Environment Canada gauge 05DF001, North Saskatchewan River at Edmonton, is at the Low Level Bridge (Alberta hydrology baseline). The Water Office lists the station at 53°32'13" N, 113°29'07" W. The Meteorological Service of Canada record for the station gives the vertical datum as ASSUMED DATUM. A gauge height on that datum is not a height above sea level. No source used here converts a gauge reading into a geodetic deck elevation.

The City's August 2026 rendering note is about a proposed replacement upstream of these spans. It says that future deck would be set high enough for the Edmonton Riverboat and for flood freeboard. It gives no number. It is not a measurement of the spans that are there.

**Rehabilitation that shows.** Northbound, 2006: about 40 percent of the main truss members were replaced, the bridge was repainted, the sidewalk was rebuilt wider, and the concrete deck was resurfaced (2008 paper). The new members are 350WT steel of the same cross-section, with the lattice pattern plasma-cut into the web, and the connections are bolts. The work was required not to show once it was finished. The truss outline was kept. The 1980 move of the east truss is the change that widened the northbound span.

Southbound: a major rehabilitation in 1994 included strengthening and painting (City memo, 31 May 2024). The City's project page says the 2025 work cleaned corrosion, strengthened and replaced steel members, and used scaffolding under each of the spans. That work finished in December 2025. The page does not describe a new truss outline. The May 2024 memo had said the southbound span would be maintained rather than rehabilitated, for a period estimated at up to 10 years. The 2025 repairs were then done. Both are on the record.

The May 2026 report says that once a replacement bridge is in service, vehicles will leave these spans, the southbound span will be demolished, and the northbound span will be kept for active use or demolished, as Council decides. Both spans are in service now. The example in that report is a haunched steel I-girder on a new alignment upstream. That girder is not either Low Level span, and it is not the James MacDonald Bridge.

The northbound span is on the City's Inventory of Historic Resources (May 2024 memo and May 2026 report). The southbound span has no heritage designation (same sources). The 2008 paper says the northbound span is on the City's 'A' register of protected structures. HistoricBridges says the bridge was designated a Municipal Historic Resource. The May 2026 report uses that designation for the High Level Bridge, Bylaw 11114, and does not use it for the Low Level. Both wordings are published. CSCE marks the 1900 bridge and the Edmonton, Yukon and Pacific Railway as a National Historic Civil Engineering Site. No Low Level Bridge record was found on HistoricPlaces.ca. The search returned the High Level Bridge.

The railway is gone. The 1900 span was built for trains. The first train crossed on 20 October 1902 (CSCE; Structurae). Wikipedia says the track was the Edmonton, Yukon and Pacific Railway, added in 1902. The 2008 paper says the design load was Grand Trunk Pacific trains, with that track on the centreline and an Edmonton Radial Railway streetcar track off the centreline. Streetcars ran from 1908 to 1939, and trolley buses from 1939 to 1965 (Wikipedia, citing Hatcher and Schwarzkopf). The paper says the streetcar track came off around 1939 and the remaining railway was abandoned circa 1950. Wikipedia and the CSCE page say the railway track was removed in 1954. Both years are published. The paper says the 1948 twin left the original span carrying northbound traffic only, and the new span carrying southbound traffic.

## Approaches

The north end is Rossdale and downtown. The City names Rossdale Road and McDougall Hill Road there. The south end is Cloverdale. The City names Connors Road and Scona Road there. The OpenStreetMap ways that leave the decks in this extract are named Connors Road or McDougall Hill. Scona Road and Rossdale Road are not the names on those ways.

No source gives the present gradient, the elevation change, or the point where either road meets natural grade, for either span. The ways below match the live OpenStreetMap node counts on 14 October 2026. A complete way is still only the mapped polyline. Its far node is not a surveyed grade point.

**North.** The hill is McDougall Hill. The two spans do not share one approach way.

Southbound span, the west deck. Way 1433098757 is McDougall Hill Road NW onto the deck: tagged one lane, `maxheight` tag 5.2, 54.5 m. Way 303665637 continues that road: tagged two lanes, `maxheight` tag 5.2, 86.3 m. Node order on both is toward the bridge.

Northbound span, the east deck. Way 22942137 is a short Connors Road way off the truss, tagged two lanes, 10.2 m. Way 303665641 is McDougall Hill Road NW from there: tagged two lanes, `maxheight` tag 5.0, 127.4 m. Node order is away from the bridge.

Way 106711221, 180.2 m, is a one-lane ramp from that north end up toward Grierson Hill. Way 106711225, 102.7 m, is the cloverleaf between McDougall Hill and Grierson Hill. Grierson Hill Road, and the span OpenStreetMap calls Grierson Bridge (way 106711211), are a separate structure on the north bank, west of this abutment. They are not in `geometry.json`.

**South.** The mapped road is Connors Road. The City also names Scona Road at this end.

Southbound span. Way 1433098760 leaves the deck: tagged one lane, 76.9 m. Node order is away from the bridge. 99A Street NW meets this way.

Northbound span. Way 1095591592 comes onto the deck: tagged two lanes, 22.7 m. Way 31883773 continues Connors Road: tagged two lanes, `maxheight` tag 4.6, destination City Centre, 146.5 m. Node order is toward the bridge.

Ways 106711192, 106711212, and 1223720740 are one-lane links between those Connors Road ways. They are not a river span. Concrete bridges in the south-approach photographs belong to the interchange on that bank. They are not Low Level decks. Where a photograph shows a concrete girder across the river behind the trusses, that girder is the James MacDonald Bridge.

## Deck

**Elevation.** Missing above sea level, for both spans. The vertical datum is missing. Gauge 05DF001 uses an assumed datum. It was not converted.

**Thickness.** Not published for either span. On the northbound span the 2006 work kept the 1980 concrete deck and replaced the top 50 mm with a silica fume concrete overlay (2008 paper). The conclusion of that paper says the deck was resurfaced. The 50 mm is the overlay. It is not the depth from the wearing surface to the soffit. A chloride reading at about 45 mm depth is not a thickness. A southbound deck thickness is not published.

**Width.** A clear roadway width is not published for either span. HistoricBridges gives the roadway width as not available. The 7925 mm figure is the northbound truss centreline spacing after 1980. The mapped outline is 23.4 m across the north end and 23.5 m across the south end. That is both decks together. It is the OpenStreetMap outline, not a published structural width.

**Lanes.** Two on each span, one direction each, asphalt, tagged 50 km/h. Four lanes together. Northbound traffic is on the east deck. Southbound traffic is on the west deck.

The May 2024 memo gives about 22,000 vehicles a day on the northbound span and about 15,000 on the southbound span, from 2022 counts. Wikipedia's infobox says 40,205 a day in 2023 and does not split it. The May 2026 report says the pair typically carries 30,000 to 40,000 a day. Those counts were not added together.

**Sidewalks.** One on each span, and none between them (2008 paper). The northbound sidewalk is on the east side. The paper says it shelters the east bottom chord, and that the west bottom chord, facing the gap, has no sidewalk.

The 2006 sidewalk on the northbound span is nearly twice as wide as the sidewalk it replaced. Neither width is given in metres. The 2015 photograph shows that sidewalk in concrete, outside the truss, with a lattice railing. The City's project page, for work planned in 2027, says the timber pedestrian decking will be replaced. The May 2026 report says the 2027 work includes sidewalk repairs. The concrete surface in the 2015 photograph and the City's timber decking are both published. The 2027 work has not been done.

A southbound sidewalk width is not published. OpenStreetMap way 302605430 is a concrete footway on the west side of the southbound centreline, which is the outer side. During the 2025 southbound work the City kept walking and cycling on the northbound sidewalk.

OpenStreetMap way 302605469, on the east side of the northbound centreline, is tagged asphalt. The 2015 photograph shows concrete. The asphalt tag is the mapper tag.

**Crossfall and grade.** Not published for either span.

**maxheight.** Way 22916473, the southbound deck, is tagged `maxheight=5.2`. The McDougall Hill ways onto that deck are tagged 5.2. The McDougall Hill way off the northbound deck is tagged 5.0. Connors Road way 31883773, onto the northbound deck, is tagged 4.6. No published source found states 5.2 m, 5.0 m, or 4.6 m. None of those tags is a deck elevation.

## Still missing

- Deck elevation above sea level, and the vertical datum, for both spans. Soffit elevation, for both spans.
- Clearance from the roadway or the soffit down to the river, and a navigation clearance, for both spans. The 13 m figure is the 1915 flood above low water. Wikipedia says that flood peaked just below the deck. The 13 m is not a clearance and it is not a geodetic elevation. Gauge 05DF001 is an assumed datum.
- Truss depth, for both spans.
- A panel count stated for the southbound span. HistoricBridges states 7 panels on the page whose length is the northbound 213.1 m. The 2008 paper does not give a panel count.
- Southbound span lengths one by one. The 211.7 m figure is an infobox total. The article text does not cite it.
- Pier heights, for both spans. A published pier count and a foundation for the southbound span alone. The northbound count is three concrete piers on timber piles. The plan size of those piles, and of the footings, is not published.
- Deck thickness, for both spans. The northbound 50 mm figure is the 2006 overlay, not the deck.
- Clear roadway width, lane width, and sidewalk width in metres, for both spans. The 7925 mm figure is the northbound truss centreline spacing after 1980. The metre width that the 2006 paper calls nearly twice the old northbound sidewalk.
- Whether the timber pedestrian decking in the City's 2027 note is the concrete surface in the 2015 photograph. That replacement has not been done.
- Crossfall and longitudinal grade, for both spans.
- Where McDougall Hill, Connors Road, Rossdale Road, and Scona Road meet natural grade, and the length, gradient, and elevation change of each climb. The OpenStreetMap lengths are plan lengths of the mapped ways.
- A published confirmation of the OpenStreetMap tags `maxheight=5.2`, `5.0`, and `4.6`. They are not deck elevations.
- A published width for the southbound sidewalk. A published confirmation of the asphalt tag on the east sidewalk way. The 2015 photograph shows concrete.
