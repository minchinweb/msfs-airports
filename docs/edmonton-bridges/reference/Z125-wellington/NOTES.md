# Z125 Wellington Bridge — modeling brief

102 Avenue across the ravine, between Churchill Crescent and Wellington Crescent, west of Groat Road. Centre used by the project: 53.5431, -113.5481. City bridge B003. The April 2024 drawing titles it B499. Those two numbers were not merged.

## What it is

The City calls the ravine Ramsey Ravine. The drawing spells Ramsay. Alberta's row says Wellington Ravine. The project list says Groat Ravine. All three names are in the sources.

This is not G117. G117 is 102 Avenue over Groat Road, to the east. This is not the river span. Do not reuse the river span's lengths, the east path width, or the pier notes.

Model the finished clear-span girder. The 1932 arch is what is being replaced. On 14 October 2026 the road way is still tagged highway=construction and access=private, so the closure is what is on the ground in that map.

## Bridge

**What is there now.** The City page says the bridge was built in 1932, four lanes, two each way, with a sidewalk on each side. It was last rehabilitated more than 25 years ago. Shoring has been in place since 2014. Replacement started the week of 15 September 2025. The March 2026 update says completion is anticipated at the end of 2026, with landscaping in 2027. The bridge is closed to all traffic, including the trail underneath.

Alberta's row for WELLINGTON (B003) prints year 1910 and RV 3, described as WELL. RAVINE BRIDGE / WELLINGTON RAVINE. The City says 1932. Both years are kept. The 3 matches the City's three spans for the old arch. The other numbers on the row were not assigned.

**Old type.** Three-span concrete arch, with piers, and shoring since 2014 (City page; EIA amendment, Associated Engineering, 8 August 2024). May 2021 options on that amendment were a single-span steel girder, a three-span haunched concrete girder, and a modern concrete arch. Detailed design of the arch started, paused in February 2022, and restarted in January 2024.

**Finished type.** Clear-span girder. The City chose a girder over a concrete arch because of cost, foundations, and schedule. The finished bridge described on the City page has some historical elements at roadway level, a shared pathway on the north, a sidewalk on the south, wider lanes, barriers, handrails, and decorative streetlights. The August 2024 amendment says both steel and concrete girders were being considered, and the permanent footprint should be reduced because the piers are eliminated. The construction footprint was unchanged.

The April 2025 request for qualifications offered Option A, a clear-span steel girder, or Option B, a NU2400 girder, with drawings for both. Closure was anticipated at the end of August 2025, and completion in fall 2026. No public award naming which option was built was found. Alberta Major Projects lists a clear-span girder, schedule 2025–2026, $29 million.

**Spans.** Old: three. Finished: one clear span, no piers. A published structural length was not found.

**Width.** Three different figures, not averaged.

The 2023 City update, when the recommendation was still a concrete arch, says a 4.2 m shared-use path on the north and a 2.8 m sidewalk on the south. The March 2026 girder text does not restate those widths. The 4.2 m is this proposal. It is not the river bridge's east path.

The April 2024 drawing, marked PRELIMINARY and FOR DISCUSSION NOT FOR CONSTRUCTION, gives an out-to-out width of 23.78 m and a right-of-way of 30.6 m. The August 2024 memo says the permanent footprint should be reduced. A revised width was not found. 23.78 m was not reduced by arithmetic.

OpenStreetMap way 37749246 tags width 12.4. No published source found states 12.4 m. The mapped outline, way 1227946189, has long sides of 70.5 m and short sides of 16.6 m. Those are mapped edges. They were not matched to 23.78 m or to 12.4. The perimeter, 174.2 m, is not a span.

**Length.** The mapped road centreline is 72.3 m horizontally. It is not a structural length of the arch or of the girder.

## Approaches

102 Avenue is closed through the site. Mapped construction approaches:

- Way 37749245, 9.1 m, width tag 14.
- Way 1462636302, 113.3 m, width tag 15.

The 113.3 m is this mapped way. It is not the 113 m length of the 102 Avenue bridge over Groat Road. The width tags are not published widths.

The north shared path continues as way 759487867, 11.1 m, and way 842281119, 9.4 m. Churchill Crescent and Wellington Crescent are the cross streets at the ends. They are not the ravine climb. No gradient is published. No far node is a surveyed grade point.

## Deck

**Elevation.** Not published. Gauge 05DF001 was not converted. No clearance down to the ravine floor was found.

**Crossfall.** The April 2024 drawing shows a proposed road crossfall of 2.5 percent on the deck, increasing to 4.0 percent, then a parabolic crown over 10 to 15 m. The drawing is marked not for construction. It is not an as-built girder crossfall.

**Lanes.** The existing bridge has four lanes (City). The finished page says wider lanes and does not give a count. Way 37749246 is tagged four lanes and 50 km/h. Node order is west to east. A live read also has construction=secondary.

**Paths.** Way 842281120, 102 Avenue NW MUP, concrete, foot and bicycle designated, 73.0 m, north of the road. Way 845134789, construction, 68.7 m, south of the road. The finished description is a shared pathway on the north and a sidewalk on the south. The 2023 widths are 4.2 m and 2.8 m, from the arch-era update.

## Still missing

- Which girder was built, steel or NU2400.
- A published span length.
- Deck elevation and clearance to the ravine.
- A girder-design width. The 4.2 m and 2.8 m are the 2023 update. The 23.78 m is the April 2024 preliminary width, later described as due to be reduced.
- Girder depth, barrier height, and the historical elements at roadway level, beyond the City's list of names.
- An openly licensed photograph of the arch or of the new girder.
