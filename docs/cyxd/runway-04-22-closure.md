# When runway 04/22 at CYXD closed

Researched 2026-10-14. No NOTAM, council motion, or news report was found that gives the day the whole runway was withdrawn. The strip this project calls 04/22 was still an operating runway in 1978, published as 03/21. It was already out of the operating inventory when Edmonton Airports wrote its 1995 strategic plan, which budgeted pavement overlays for "the two runways."

That is not the 3 August 2010 closure of runway 16/34, and not the 30 November 2013 closure of runway 12/30 and of the airport.

## The strip

04/22 in `CYXD.xml` is the northeast–southwest runway, the same pavement as the wartime and 1970s runway 3/21 (later 03/21). It is not 12/30 and not 16/34.

Companion text page saved as `departures/rcaf-on-track-1978-edmonton-muni-1.jpg`. Variation 23° E, elevation 2,200 ft. |

The scenery heading is 57.78° true. Magnetic heading is that true heading minus easterly variation, and the runway number is the magnetic heading rounded to the nearest 10°.

- 1942, variation 25° E: about 33° magnetic, runway 03. Matches the published 3/21.
- 1978, variation 23° E: about 35° magnetic, still on the runway-03 side of the rounding boundary. The chart still labels it 03/21.
- The Canada Flight Supplement effective 20 November 2008 prints variation as `Adj 17°E` (local copy cited below). The same true heading would then be about 41° magnetic, runway 04.

No Canada Flight Supplement or airport directory turned up that actually publishes the designator **04/22**. The name in this project is the later magnetic number of the 03/21 pavement. The runway may have been closed while it was still numbered 03/21. The closure date below is the date for that pavement, under whichever number was current.

## 1978: the west end was closed, the runway was not

The 1978 plan prints "Closed" on the pavement west of the runway 03 threshold, beside taxiways W and Z. That is the stub before a displaced threshold, not a closed runway. The same page still publishes landing distances for both directions:

- Runway 03 landing 3,814 ft, so the threshold is displaced 637 ft (4,451 − 3,814).
- Runway 21 landing 4,319 ft, displaced 132 ft.

Right-hand circuits are listed for runways 21, 29, and 34, so runway 21 was still in the circuit pattern. The text page's noise rules name 16-34, runway 11 for takeoff, and runway 29. They do not mention 03/21, which fits a short crosswind runway that was published but not preferred.

## Before 1995: only two runways were still being maintained

The Office of the City Auditor's *Edmonton City Centre Airport Audit* (19 November 2003) describes the operator's 1995 plan in these words:

> In its 1995 strategic plan, Edmonton Airports indicated that it planned average capital investments of about $1.5 million per year from 1997 to 2000, totalling approximately $6 million. Of these capital investments, major overlays of the two runways were planned at a total cost of $3 million.

PDF: [03079_City_Centre_Airport.pdf](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/assets/PDF/03079_City_Centre_Airport.pdf), section "City Centre Airport Safety Status." The plan is *Edmonton Airports Strategic Plan: Airports 2000* (1995). The auditor's footnote 16 points the related capital figure at Appendix B, page 21, of that plan. The 1995 plan itself was not found online, so this is the auditor's account of it, not a quotation from the plan.

An overlay budget for "the two runways" means 03/21 was already outside the set of runways Edmonton Airports intended to keep. The closure of the whole runway is therefore before 1995, not a 1995 event. The 1995 text does not say which calendar year the third runway was withdrawn.

## Later sources that omit it

These confirm the runway was already gone. They do not tighten the 1978–1995 gap.

- AOPA Airport Directory, 2007/2008 edition, CYXD entry. Only runway 12-30 (5,868 x 200 ft, MIRL) and runway 16-34 (5,700 x 200 ft, HIRL). [international_ops.pdf](https://www.aopa.org/-/media/Files/AOPA/Home/Flight-Planning/International-Flying/International/AOPA-Online-For-Members-AOPA-Pilot-Information-Center-Canada/international_ops.pdf). Noted in `fbos.md`.
- Canada Flight Supplement effective 20 November 2008 (through 15 January 2009). Local copy `charts/procedures-2010/2008-CFS-CYXD-B230-B231.pdf`. Runway data lists only 12(121°)/30(301°), 5,868 x 200 ft asphalt, and 16(163°)/34(343°), 5,700 x 200 ft asphalt. Preferential runway order: departures 30, 34, 16, 12; arrivals 12, 16, 30, 34. A second copy of a CFS aerodrome section is in the same folder as `CFS_AD_E-ivao.pdf` ([IVAO CFS_AD_E.pdf](https://www.ivao.ca/charts/CFS/CFS_AD_E.pdf)).
- The [airports-worldwide CYXD entry](http://www.airports-worldwide.com/canada/alberta/edmonton_city_centre_alberta.php) likewise lists only 12/30 and 16/34. Its variation field is 016° E as of 01/06.
- Wikimedia Commons aerial, 6 June 2012, `aerials/Edmonton_City_Centre_Airport.jpg`. The field shows the two crossing runways only. The diagonal pavement is not there, so the strip had been removed, not merely closed and left marked, by that date. Removal and operational closure are different events. Operational closure is the pre-1995 bound above. Physical removal is only bounded as sometime before 6 June 2012.
- The 8 February 2009 oblique (`aerials/Edmonton_-_City_Centre_Municipal_-_Blatchford_Field_YXD_-_CYXD_AN1479402.jpg`) is too distant, and the infield is snow-covered, to count pavement strips from it.

Ken Tingley's historical impact assessment (June 2009, City report 2009DCM032 Attachment 6) discusses the northeast–southwest runway in the 1939–c.1940 construction work, including a crown for drainage and approach lights on both ends. It does not give a closure date. [2009DCM032-Att6.pdf](https://webdocs.edmonton.ca/occtopusdocs/Public/Complete/Reports/Ex/CSAM/2009-06-24/2009DCM032-Att6.pdf).

## Dates that belong to the other runways

City of Edmonton news release, council direction to close the airport at 23:59 on 30 November 2013: [news-archive-487808-72f8.pdf](https://webdocs.edmonton.ca/news_archives/news-archive-487808-72f8.pdf).

- Runway 16/34 closed to air traffic on 3 August 2010. A NOTAM was issued at 03:00 local that morning. The same release says the July 2009 council decision was a phased closure and that 16/34 was the runway closed in that first phase. Wikipedia's "October 2010" wording for a permanent closure of 16/34 is a later description of the same runway, not a second event, and not 04/22.
- Runway 12/30 was the last runway. Barricades and closed markings went on it on 1 December 2013, after the lights went out at 16:49 on 30 November. [Commemoration advisory](https://webdocs.edmonton.ca/news_archives/news-archive-513911-d314.pdf).

The 2009 motion and the 2013 closure papers name only those two runways. They have nothing to say about 03/21 or 04/22 because that strip was already out of the operating set.

## What was searched and not found

Public web search, the 2003 auditor's report, the 2009 Tingley assessment, the 1978 On Track pages, the 2007/08 AOPA directory, and the 20 November 2008 CFS. No source gave a calendar date, a NOTAM number, or a council minute for withdrawing the whole of 03/21 / 04/22. Newspaper and city-archives searches did not return a usable hit (the archives site blocked the browser check). The 1995 *Airports 2000* plan was not found as its own PDF.

## Second search, 14 October 2026

Looked for a source inside 1979–1994 that either still lists 03/21 or already counts two runways. The operating-closure window did not move.

Checked, and what each one actually says:

- Environment Council of Alberta, *Public Hearings on Noise in Alberta*, July 1982 ([PDF](https://nonoise.org/epa%20index/roll12/roll12doc53.pdf)). Calls Edmonton Municipal the province's worst noise source because of Boeing 737s, and recommends banning regular large-jet use. The report text does not list runways, so it does not say whether 03/21 was still open.
- Alberta Land Compensation Board, *E-Z Air Inc. v Edmonton (City)*, 2018 ABLCB 7, para. 24 ([PDF](https://www.meritvaluations.ca/_files/ugd/32ec9c_b5389b67dcc647719d1b850658866b64.pdf)): "Until 1996 scheduled air service was provided utilizing two runways." That matches the 1995 plan. It does not say when the airport went from three runways to two.
- Internet Archive full-text search for "edmonton muni" plus "runway": no documents.
- Google Books was blocked. Alberta On Record did not return a readable result. AvCanada and PPRuNe threads about YXD discuss the 2013 closure, not 03/21.
- Shawn Roberts, "Final for 16 at Edmonton City Center," 8 October 2007 ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Final_for_16_at_Edmonton_City_Center_(1526811765).jpg)). The frame is the north end of runway 16. The crossing runways are not in the picture, so it does not show whether the diagonal pavement was still there.

A dated Canada Flight Supplement or air photo from 1979 through 1994 would close the gap. Until one of those turns up, the honest answer is: operating in 1978 as runway 03/21, with only the west-end stub closed, and no longer one of the airport's runways by the 1995 strategic plan.

## Searched as Edmonton Industrial Airport

The Industrial name does not cover the closure window. It is the name used after the city took the field back from the wartime lease and before the mid-1970s rename. By the 1978 *On Track* guide the field is already "Edmonton Muni," and runway 03/21 is still an operating runway. A closure notice filed under the Industrial name would have to predate that guide.

Name dates found under that search:

- A July 1996 *Info Canadian* timeline, reprinted by Pacific Western retirees ([pwareunion.com/interesting.htm](http://www.pwareunion.com/interesting.htm)), says city council renamed Blatchford Field the Industrial Airport on 13 November 1963, and renamed it Edmonton Municipal Airport on 15 May 1975. The same timeline's 1963 sentence is garbled ("city council Jornis changed"), so the day is from that reprint, not from a council minute read here. The same list has no runway closure. It does record a "comprehensive airport management program" adopted by council on 9 October 1984, which sits inside the 1978–1995 gap, but the program text was not found.
- Aviation Safety Network's YXD history page dates the reversion to Municipal Airport as 1979, without a day ([asn.flightsafety.org](https://asn.flightsafety.org/database/airport/id/YXD)). That is a year later than the *Info Canadian* date. Either way the Industrial name was gone by the time the 1978 guide, or within a year of it.
- A Hangar 11 heritage report says the field was renamed Edmonton Industrial Airport in the early 1960s and Edmonton City Centre Airport about three decades later. It does not mention 03/21 or 04/22. [Document 78490](https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=78490).
- A 1975 University of Alberta thesis uses the title "Edmonton's industrial airport" (Hans-Werner Mary, noise and house prices). The full text was not online. It is from the rename year, so it cannot date a later closure.

No Industrial-era source listed a closure of runway 3/21 or 03/21. The operating-closure bound stays after 1978 and before 1995.
