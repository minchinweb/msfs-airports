## What the photos show
### Hangar 39
- Very large hangar, ribbed metal cladding: **pale (sky) blue** on the upper gable face and the corner wall, **white** panels (a large panel above the doors and at the lower corner), and a **mid-blue base band**.
- **Door wall** (faces south, per the captions): tall folded or sliding leaves, white ribbed, each with two horizontal bands of beige louvre or glazed panels, green guide posts, a dark door head under a canopy line; the wall has two bays (a taller main bay, and a lower second bay to its right) separated by a low pale-blue block with personnel doors, a white roller door, and a mesh-screen truss at the top.
- Low-pitch gable roof with a white fascia; the roundel **"39"** at the corner next to the doors and a smaller one on the north wall.
- **North side:** a two-storey pale-blue wing with a ribbon of small windows and a fenced yard.

### Hangar 38 (Building 38)
- **Mustard / olive-yellow** ribbed cladding, low-pitch gable roof with dark trim, long wall facing south with a big door panel (cream edge trim), personnel doors, tall narrow dark-framed windows at the left end, the "38" roundel in the upper part with a small canopy below it.
- Stands west of Hangar 39 across a small yard (container, parked equipment), per the photos taken from the south.

## Notes for the model
- Door/gable orientation and the exact footprints must be checked against OSM, the 1944 aerial and the 1980s photos; these photos are from 2014.
- Era: the building dates and the end of use of the PWA hangar were not checked. PWA hangars would fit the 1960s-80s part of the scenery's range; confirm from the 1980 archive photos (`City of Edmonton Archive photos/1980`, `1980 ca`) before placing it.
- Colours: Hangar 39 pale blue + white + mid-blue; Hangar 38 mustard yellow; both carry the same blue numbered discs used at the end of the airport's life.
