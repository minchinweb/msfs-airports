# CYXD approach, arrival, and departure procedures

Searched 13 October 2026 from public web sources only. Edmonton City Centre / Blatchford Field (CYXD / YXD) closed 30 November 2013, so NAV CANADA does not sell a current plate. The procedure set below is the Canada Air Pilot cycle **0901Z 11 February 2010 to 0901Z 8 April 2010**, plus the Canada Flight Supplement cycle **0901Z 20 November 2008 to 0901Z 15 January 2009**. These are expired. They are not for navigation.

## Approaches (CAP 3, 11 Feb 2010–8 Apr 2010)

Field elevation **2202**. Variation on the ILS plate is **17°E**. Coordinates in the title block are **N53°34.21′ W113°31.14′**. Common communications on the plates: Legacy AWOS 128.625, ATIS 125.4, arrival/departure 119.5, tower 119.1 (outside tower hours, Red Deer radio 119.1, MF 5 NM), ground 121.9. Safe altitude 100 NM is **6700**. All thresholds are displaced; see the aerodrome chart. ARCAL is 119.1 type K. Right-hand circuits on runways 30 and 34.

## Departure

### EDMONTON City FOUR DEP (CYXD 4.) — page 98

Standard instrument departure, charted as **SID (VECTOR)**.

Communications: Legacy AWOS 128.625, ATIS 125.4, clearance delivery **121.3**, ground 121.9, tower 119.1, outside tower hours Red Deer radio 119.1 (MF 5 NM), departure **119.5**.

Runway headings printed on the plate: **34 = 343°**, **16 = 163°**, **30 = 301°**, **12 = 121°**.

The instruction box reads:

- All runways: climb runway heading for vectors to the assigned route or a depicted fix. Maintain **7000 ft ASL** or the flight-planned altitude, whichever is lower.
- Runway 12: minimum climb gradient **390 ft/NM to 2800 ft ASL**
- Runway 16: minimum climb gradient **250 ft/NM to 2800 ft ASL**
- Runway 30: minimum climb gradient **360 ft/NM to 2500 ft ASL**
- Refer to the noise-abatement procedures for the rest

The plan view is a vector SID onto fixes around the YEG VOR (117.6, channel 123), with restricted areas CYR 265 (surface to below 3400, continuous) and CYR 212 (surface to 3200, continuous). The clearest fix names on the small screenshot are **ANOUK**, **ABENY**, and **NUPPS**. Use the saved plate for the remaining names, radials, and coordinates. The chart is marked not to scale.

Communication failure, if recognized 10 minutes or less after takeoff in IFR weather:

1. Transponder 7600
2. At the last assigned altitude, proceed directly on course
3. Maintain that altitude for 10 minutes after takeoff, then
4. Climb to the flight-planned altitude

If the failure is recognized more than 10 minutes after takeoff, use the enroute communication-failure procedure.

The same climb gradients are repeated on the aerodrome chart (page 100), with the runway heading, and with a specified-visual alternative: climb visually to 2800 ft before proceeding on course on runways 12 and 16, and to 2500 ft on runway 30. Take-off minima are boxed on that chart for runways 34, 12, 16, and 30.

## Noise abatement, which is the published arrival and departure procedure — page 99

The page footer says effective **8 June 2006**, change “Editorial”, inside the 11 Feb 2010 book.

**Restrictions.** Some operations are restricted **2200–0700 local**. Permission in those hours goes to aircraft that meet the airport noise limits (telephone 780-477-2442). Engine run-ups, other than those that are part of the pre-takeoff check, are prohibited 2200–0700 local.

**Preferential runways**, consistent with safety. ATC assigns runways so as many arrivals and departures as possible avoid noise-sensitive areas.

- Departures: **30, then 34, then 16, then 12**
- Arrivals: **12, then 16, then 30, then 34**
- From **0000–0600 local**, use runways **12 and 30** unless traffic, wind, weather, or runway condition requires another runway

**Departures.** IFR aircraft climb on runway heading to **3200 ft ASL** before proceeding on course. Turbojets climb to **4500 ft ASL**, except **4000 ft ASL** on runway 30. VNAP A or B applies on all runways.

**Arrivals.** IFR aircraft do not descend below **3200 ft ASL** until established on the glide path or VASIS. Turbojets use **4500 ft ASL**. Circling is excepted. ATC may clear an unrestricted descent if those altitudes are kept. On an IFR or visual approach to **runway 16**, do not descend below **3400 ft ASL** until past the **XD NDB** inbound.

The 20 November 2008 CFS (pages B230–B231) prints the same runway order, the same 3200 / 4500 / 4000 climb, the same 2200–0700 restriction, and the same runway-16 note about 3400 ft and the XD NDB. It also says ab initio training is not allowed in the control zone, a transponder is required in the control zone, and right-hand circuits apply on runways 30 and 34.

Transport Canada’s *Canadian Aviation Regulations* section 602.106, current to 21 September 2026, still lists **Edmonton City Centre (Blatchford Field) Airport, all runways**, as noise-restricted for a subsonic turbo-jet over 34 000 kg (74,956 lb) unless a noise-compliance certificate is carried. The footnote says the table was taken from the Canada Flight Supplement. The airport is closed; the schedule entry is still on the books. Source: [SOR/96-433, s. 602.106](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-96-433/page-62.html).

## Aerodrome chart numbers used by the procedures — page 100

The declared-distance table is on the saved aerodrome chart. TORA matches the CFS runway lengths: **5868 ft** for 12/30 and **5700 ft** for 16/34. LDA matches the displaced thresholds, which are also in the 2008 CFS: runway 12 displaced 800 ft (LDA **5068**), runway 30 displaced 1187 ft (LDA **4681**), runway 16 displaced 500 ft (LDA **5200**), runway 34 displaced 540 ft (LDA **5160**). TODA and ASDA are printed in that same table; the screenshot type is too small to quote those two rows here. All runways restricted to 140,000 lb. Threshold elevations on the sketch: runway 16 area **2192**, runway 34 **2181**. Taxiway W is uncontrolled. The CFS adds runway 30 upslope 0.36%, and lighting: runway 34 has a non-standard 2200-foot approach lighting system.

## What exists in other sources, without a plate image

[PilotNav’s CYXD page](https://www.pilotnav.com/airport/CYXD) still indexes the same 2010 set: aerodrome chart, the helicopter VTPC, two CFS aerodrome pages, NDB RWY 16 (GNSS), LOC (BC)/DME RWY 16, LOC/DME RWY 34, ILS or NDB RWY 34 (GNSS), EDMONTON City FOUR DEP (CYXD 4.), and a separate noise-abatement entry. Those links go to a Dauntless Simplates sales page, not to a downloadable plate.

[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_City_Centre_Airport) and the matching airports-worldwide summary say that by the time the airport closed, runway 12 had an **RNAV (GNSS) approach to LPV** minima and runway 30 had an **RNAV (GNSS) approach to LNAV** minima, and that after runway 16/34 closed the old NDB approaches to 16 and 34 remained only to circling minima. Runway 16/34 was closed to traffic by NOTAM at 03:00 on **3 August 2010**, after the Indy and Airfest; Wikipedia also describes the closure as permanent in October 2010. No RNAV (GNSS) RWY 12 or RWY 30 plate was found to download. The February 2010 CAP scan above is from before that runway closure, and it does not contain those RNAV approaches.

No CYXD STAR was found. Arrival routing into the field in this material is the instrument approach, the noise-abatement altitude, or the VFR terminal chart.
