## Possible uses
- `PC013720`: the 1950 City Archives photo EA-10-838 already shows the same Administration Building; this one is a clearer colour view of the wings, roofs and drive for the historic building model.
- `PC013987`: the two-hangar group and towers match EA-160-398 / EA-160-402 (1938); the curved terminal and apron are a better view than the 1944 aerial.
- `WCWdb_2534.pdf`: a dated plan to check runway and building positions against the 1944 aerial and the runway-04 alignment question in `STATUS.md`. Not georeferenced; nothing has been measured from it yet.

## Which hangars are in PC013987? (compared 2026-10-17)
Compared against City Archives EA-160-398, 402, 466, the 1944 National Archives aerial (`../National Archives/RG-18-AA-208-27-ac.jpg`) and the Northwest Industries photo (`../hangar11-photos/flickr_woodysworld1_11393738936.jpg`).
- **Near hangar (closest to the apron and terminal): the 1938 hangar.** Barrel vault with a brick tower at each end (one tall and stepped, one small). EA-160-398 (distant) and EA-160-402 (scaffolded, towers at both ends) show the same form. High confidence.
- **Far hangar (NW, beside the road): not identified.** It is larger than the 1938 hangar: a barrel vault with a long pale two-storey flat-roofed wing along its front, a brick tower at the far end and a small red-roofed building at its front. EA-160-402 shows a second long barrel-roofed block to the right of the scaffolded hangar, which may be it. EA-160-466 (1938) shows a different building: a small arched hangar with a tower at the left, a three-storey block in the middle, and a large low-pitched hipped/gabled hangar at the right. I could not match the far hangar to that either.
- **1944 aerial:** west of the circular drive, between Kingsway and the apron, there is one large dark-roofed hangar with a pale strip along its front and a smaller building nearer the circle. This fits the postcard pair (small = 1938 hangar nearest the terminal, large = the far hangar), but the 1944 photo has not been scaled, so the sizes are not measured.
- **Northwest Industries / Hangar 11: no match.** The 1956 Northwest Industries photo shows a long multi-storey ribbon-windowed block with a rooftop control cab and a big sign, and a white barrel vault at its right. That is the USAAF T3 (Hangar 11) on the **east** edge of the field. The postcard's hangars are on the west side by the terminal and have brick towers and a lower wing. Neither postcard hangar is Hangar 11. Whether a west-side hangar later housed Northwest Industries is unknown.
- **Not resolved:** the far hangar's identity, its size, and what it was used for.
