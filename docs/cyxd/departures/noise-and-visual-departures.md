# Noise abatement, preferential runway, and visual departures — CYXD

Scope: Edmonton Municipal / Edmonton Industrial / Edmonton City Centre / Blatchford Field (CYXD / YXD), 1940 through closure on 30 November 2013. Edmonton International (CYEG) procedures are excluded wherever a source is about that airport.

No Canada Flight Supplement (CFS) or Canada Air Pilot (CAP) page for CYXD was retrieved as text or as an image this session. Nothing below is a published heading, climb gradient, or “no turn before” altitude. Where a project note is the only transcription of a primary page, that is labelled.

## What did the CFS and CAP print for noise, preferential runway, or departures?

### Takeaway
No CFS or CAP noise, preferential-runway, or departure paragraph for CYXD was recovered as a quotation from the publication itself. The closest operational text is a 1978 commercial pilot guide, *On Track*, which prints a short “NOISE ABATEMENT, unless otherwise directed” block, plus a 1984 city request (not re-read this session) that Transport Canada add a runway 30 preferential departure and formal takeoff procedures to the *Canada Air Pilot – West*. Secondary sources from 2006 onward say a curfew-style window and noise procedures existed, and point the reader to Nav Canada, without quoting the plate.

### Cited Findings
- **1978, *On Track: the Pilot’s Air Travel Guide*, 1st annual edition, “Edmonton Muni”.** The RCAF.Info station page states that this edition lists the aerodrome as “Edmonton Muni” and hosts photographs of the two guide pages. It does not transcribe them. — [RCAF Station Edmonton Municipal](https://rcaf.info/rcaf-stations/alberta-rcaf-stations/rcaf-station-edmonton-municipal/)
- **1978 wording, as transcribed in a project note from those *On Track* images (not re-OCR’d this session).** Under “NOISE ABATEMENT, unless otherwise directed”: (a) runway 16-34 to be used during calm conditions (no wind); (b) runway 11 take-off, aircraft gross weight restricted to 12,500 lb; (c) runway 29 landing weight 30,000 lb maximum between 04–14Z (daylight time 03–13Z). The same page says “Consult Edmonton Special Procedures Chart” and records right-hand circuits for runways 21, 29, and 34. Runways then were 03/21, 11/29, and 16/34. The Special Procedures Chart was not found. — project note `primary-sources-1941-1978.md`, citing the RCAF.Info images above. Treat the bullets as a second-hand transcription until the page images are read again.
- **9 October 1984, City of Edmonton Council Meeting No. 23 (not retrieved this session).** The same project note says the Municipal Airport Noise Policy Study asked Transport Canada to amend *Canada Air Pilot – West* so that runway 30 would be the preferential noise-abatement departure runway and runway 12 the preferential arrival runway, and asked that formal noise-abatement take-off procedures be added to the CAP. The note treats that request as evidence those items were not already in the CAP. The archives page for the minutes returned a browser-verification wall and no minute text. — [Council Meeting No. 23, 9 Oct 1984](https://cityarchives.edmonton.ca/meeting-no-23-74); transcription only in the project note above.
- **13 August 1973, Council Meeting No. 24 (not retrieved this session).** The same project note says the City and the Ministry of Transport agreed which carriers would use Industrial versus International with aircraft over 12,500 lb, the same weight as the 1978 runway 11 take-off cap. Minutes page not opened this session. — [Council Meeting No. 24, 13 Aug 1973](https://cityarchives.edmonton.ca/meeting-no-24-64)
- **December 2006 Wikipedia snapshot, while the airport was still open.** “Due to its location in the central portion of the city, there are both curfew restrictions and noise abatement procedures. The field maintains 24 X 7 operations, with the strictest noise regulations in effect from 22:00h to 07:00h local time. more information can be garnered from Edmonton Airports or from NAV CANADA.” No runway, heading, or altitude is given. — [Wikipedia, December 2006](https://wikipedia2006.classicistranieri.com/e/d/m/Edmonton_City_Centre_%28Blatchford_Field%29_Airport_e888.html)
- **Current Wikipedia (page fetched 2026; airport closed 30 November 2013).** Same claim, worded “10:00 pm to 7:00 am local time,” still with no quoted CFS or CAP departure text. Field data (elevation, runway lengths) are attributed to the Canada Flight Supplement, not the noise paragraph. — [Edmonton City Centre Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_City_Centre_Airport)
- **Flytime.ca closure article.** Repeats the 22:00–07:00 window and 24-hour operations, and says downtown buildings could not exceed 150 metres because of approach and departure clearance. That is an obstacle-height limit, not a departure procedure. — [Edmonton City Centre Airport Closure](https://flytime.ca/edmonton-city-centre-airport-closure-end-of-era/)
- **19 July 2018 CFS, after closure.** RCAF.Info says this CFS lists “Edmonton City Center (Blachford Field) AB (N53 34 W113 31)” in the abandoned list. No procedure text. — [RCAF.Info](https://rcaf.info/rcaf-stations/alberta-rcaf-stations/rcaf-station-edmonton-municipal/)
- **False neighbour, do not use.** An April 2008 CFS extract (`CFS_AD_R.pdf`) prints a noise procedure for Red Deer: right-hand circuits on runways 08 and 13, and, when the tower is closed, arrivals on 08 or 13 and departures on 26 or 31, “See CAP.” Those runway numbers are not CYXD’s. — [IVAO CFS extract, 10 Apr 2008](https://www.ivao.ca/charts/CFS/CFS_AD_R.pdf)
- **False neighbour, do not use.** Edmonton International noise text (NADP 1 or 2 on all runways; runway 12 turbojet/turbofan, no right turn west of the Leduc NDB / KEKBI; avoid Leduc below 5,000 ft ASL; runway 30 right-hand circuits) is CYEG. Boeing’s `edmonton_int.pdf` is that airport. Current Edmonton Airports noise pages are that airport. — [Boeing, Edmonton International noise PDF](https://www.boeing.com/content/dam/boeing/boeingdotcom/commercial/noise/edmonton_int.pdf); [Edmonton International aircraft noise](https://flyyeg.com/corporate/esg/aircraft-noise/)
- **Grokipedia (unreliable).** It says the airport “enforced strict noise abatement procedures, such as preferential runway use and climb restrictions, alongside a curfew from 22:00 to 07:00.” Its footnote 56 for that sentence is the Boeing Edmonton International PDF above. The preferential-runway and climb-restriction clauses are not supported by a CYXD plate in that PDF. The curfew clause matches Wikipedia. — [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Edmonton_City_Centre_Airport)

### Inferences
- The 1978 *On Track* block, if the transcription is accurate, is an operational noise rule for the field, not a track. Calm wind selects the north–south runway pair. The 12,500 lb cap keeps heavier aeroplanes off runway 11, which points toward downtown. It does not state a heading after liftoff.
- The 1984 council request, if the transcription is accurate, implies that as of that date the CAP did not yet name runway 30 as the preferential noise departure or contain a formal noise take-off procedure. Whether Transport Canada later printed either item was not found.

### Gaps
- No CFS “PRO” / “NOISE” lines, and no CAP noise-abatement or departure page, for Edmonton Municipal, Industrial, or City Centre, in any year from the 1960s through 2013, were opened.
- The *Edmonton Special Procedures Chart* named in the 1978 guide was not found.
- The 1978 page images and the 1973 and 1984 council minutes were not re-read this session. The archives site blocked an unauthenticated fetch.
- Cadet and IVAO CFS PDFs that index CYXD did not include the Alberta aerodrome-directory page for it.

## Were published headings, climb gradients, or “no turn before” rules printed for 12/30, 16/34, or the earlier numbers?

### Takeaway
No. No source quoted a heading, a climb gradient, or a “no turn before” altitude or distance for runways 12/30, 16/34, 11/29, 15/33, or 03/21 (earlier 3/21) at this airport.

### Cited Findings
- **1978 *On Track* transcription (see previous section).** The only departure-side restrictions in that block are which runway to use in calm wind, and a 12,500 lb gross-weight cap on runway 11 take-off. Right-hand circuits are listed for 21, 29, and 34. No heading, gradient, or turn altitude. — project note citing [RCAF.Info *On Track* images](https://rcaf.info/rcaf-stations/alberta-rcaf-stations/rcaf-station-edmonton-municipal/)
- **1984 request (unverified this session).** The council asked for formal noise-abatement take-off procedures to be added to the CAP. The request itself is not a published procedure, and the resulting CAP text was not found. — [Council Meeting No. 23](https://cityarchives.edmonton.ca/meeting-no-23-74), via the project note only
- **2006 TSB report A06W0111.** A Seneca II left Edmonton City Centre at 1131 MDT, VFR, planned direct to Prince George at 8,500 ft ASL, and reached 8,500 ft nine minutes after departure. The GPS then showed cruise on an average track of 260° magnetic. The report does not say what the tower assigned, what the initial heading was, or when the first turn was made. A later turn “to keep the aircraft from flying over the residential and commercial areas” was on short final at Edson, not at CYXD. — [TSB A06W0111](https://www.tsb.gc.ca/eng/rapports-reports/aviation/2006/a06w0111/a06w0111.html)
- **National Canadian default, not a CYXD plate.** Prior project notes record the modern AIM / CAP GEN expectation when no SID is assigned: cross the departure end at least 35 ft, climb runway heading, no turn below 400 ft AGL, 200 ft per NM to the IFR altitude. That rule is national. It was not found printed as a CYXD noise procedure, and the 1970s wording of the national rule was not re-checked this session. — project note `official-sids-cap-cfs.md`
- **CYEG turn restriction, excluded.** “No right turns west of the Leduc NDB” and NADP 1 or 2 are Edmonton International runway 12 / all-runway jet procedures. — [Boeing Edmonton International PDF](https://www.boeing.com/content/dam/boeing/boeingdotcom/commercial/noise/edmonton_int.pdf); [YEG noise page](https://flyyeg.com/corporate/esg/aircraft-noise/)

### Inferences
- None on headings or altitudes. A right-hand circuit on 21, 29, or 34 (1978) would put the first VFR turn on a stated side of those runways, but the guide does not say how soon after liftoff that turn starts.

### Gaps
- No published initial heading, climb gradient, or “no turn before” rule for any CYXD runway designation from 1940 through 2013.
- No CAP noise-abatement departure procedure (NADP, VNAP, or a named textual DP) for CYXD. CITY FOUR, if it was a SID, is a separate instrument departure and its plate was not found (see the SID note in the earlier folder).

## What did CAR 602.105 and 602.106 require at this airport, and did the CAP/CFS noise text go further?

### Takeaway
Section 602.105 does not name CYXD. It requires pilots to follow whatever noise procedures the Minister has put in the CAP or CFS, including preferential runways, departure procedures, and restricted hours. Section 602.106 does name the airport: every runway is noise-restricted for take-off by a subsonic turbojet heavier than 34,000 kg (74,956 lb) unless a noise-compliance certificate is on board. That is a certification gate for heavy turbojets, not a flight path. The 1978 *On Track* rules, if accurate, go further: they bind runway choice and a 12,500 lb take-off cap, and they are not limited to heavy turbojets. Whether a later CFS entry added hours, a preferential runway, or a departure track beyond 602.106 was not recovered.

### Cited Findings
- **CAR 602.105, current consolidation of SOR/96-433 (Justice Laws page fetched 2026).** “No person shall operate an aircraft at or in the vicinity of an aerodrome except in accordance with the applicable noise abatement procedures and noise control requirements specified by the Minister in the Canada Air Pilot or Canada Flight Supplement, including the procedures and requirements relating to (a) preferential runways; (b) minimum noise routes; (c) hours when aircraft operations are prohibited or restricted; (d) arrival procedures; (e) departure procedures; (f) duration of flights; (g) the prohibition or restriction of training flights; (h) VFR or visual approaches; (i) simulated approach procedures; and (j) the minimum altitude for the operation of aircraft in the vicinity of the aerodrome.” The section lists no aerodrome. — [CAR page, Justice Laws](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/sor-96-433/page-62.html)
- **CAR 602.106(1), same consolidation.** “Subject to subsection (2), no person shall operate a subsonic turbo-jet aeroplane that has a maximum certificated take-off weight of more than 34 000 kg (74,956 pounds) on take-off at a noise-restricted runway set out in column II of an item of the table to this section at an aerodrome set out in column I of that item, unless there is on board (a) a certificate of noise compliance issued in respect of the aeroplane under section 507.20; or (b) where the aeroplane is not a Canadian aircraft, a foreign certificate of noise compliance issued by the state of registry that has been validated by the Minister under section 507.23.” Paragraph (c) is repealed (SOR/2020-151, s. 14). — [CAR 602.106](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-96-433/section-602.106.html)
- **CAR 602.106(2), exceptions.** Subsection (1) does not apply to the extent it conflicts with a treaty obligation; where the pilot-in-command has declared an emergency; or on an air evacuation, any other emergency air operation, or a departure from an aerodrome where the aircraft had to land because of an emergency. — same Justice Laws pages
- **Table item 3, same consolidation.** Column I: “Edmonton City Centre (Blatchford Field) Airport”. Column II: “All runways”. The footnote says the information is taken from Nav Canada’s Canada Flight Supplement. Listed amending instruments include SOR/99-470, SOR/2004-29, SOR/2005-169, SOR/2009-167, SOR/2019-119, and SOR/2020-151. The closed airport is still in the table on the page fetched in 2026. — [CAR page](https://lois-laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/sor-96-433/page-62.html)
- **Earlier wording, reprinted in a Boeing Edmonton International noise PDF (the procedure pages of that PDF are CYEG; the regulatory appendix is the national table).** That reprint of 602.106 still allowed take-off if the aeroplane carried (a) a certificate of airworthiness stating it met the noise standards, or (b) a certificate of noise compliance, or (c) a foreign document that it met that state’s noise requirements. The table line is “3 Edmonton City Center(Blatchford Field) All runways”. The same PDF’s preferential-runway note for Edmonton International cites “Air Navigation Orders, Series II, No. 27, Schedule II” for International’s runway 12 only. The ANO schedule text itself was not retrieved. — [Boeing edmonton_int.pdf](https://www.boeing.com/content/dam/boeing/boeingdotcom/commercial/noise/edmonton_int.pdf)
- **1982, Environment Council of Alberta, *Public Hearings on Noise in Alberta* (ECA82-16/RR, July 1982).** “At the public hearings, the Edmonton Municipal Airport was identified as the single most disturbing noise source in the province. The major contributors to the noise problem are the Boeing 737 jets.” Recommendation 21: the City should give serious consideration to prohibiting regular use of Edmonton Municipal Airport by large jet aircraft; a Dash 7 could give a similar level of convenience; Toronto had banned all jets at the Island airport; the Council did not believe smaller business jets contributed enough to warrant eliminating them. The recommendation is a policy proposal. It prints no heading or altitude. — [ECA report PDF](https://nonoise.org/epa%20index/roll12/roll12doc53.pdf)
- **1986–1991, City noise monitor north of the field.** Bylaw 10240 renewed a 19 September 1986 leave and licence with the Government of Canada so the City could keep an outdoor microphone and a 7-metre pole on CFB Griesbach “for monitoring … aircraft noise which originates from Edmonton Municipal Airport.” That is a measurement site, not a departure instruction. — [Bylaw 10240](https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=123684)
- **1978 *On Track* transcription.** The 12,500 lb runway 11 take-off cap and the calm-wind runway pair apply by aircraft weight and wind, not by the 34,000 kg turbojet threshold in 602.106. A 12,500 lb aeroplane is far below a heavy turbojet. — project note citing the 1978 guide

### Inferences
- 602.105 makes a CFS or CAP noise paragraph mandatory if one was published. It does not create a heading by itself. Because no CYXD CAP/CFS departure paragraph was found, it is not shown that 602.105 imposed a turn or altitude at this airport beyond whatever unpublished or unrecovered text Nav Canada held.
- 602.106 does not ground a compliant heavy turbojet. A Boeing 737 is over 34,000 kg maximum certificated take-off weight, so it needed the noise certificate (or an exception) to depart any CYXD runway. Pacific Western and later operators did fly 737s from the field (see next section), which is consistent with those aeroplanes carrying the certificate, not with a ban on all jets.
- The 1982 council-of-the-environment recommendation and the 1978 weight cap are about which aeroplanes and which runway, not about how to turn after takeoff.

### Gaps
- The full text of Air Navigation Order Series II, No. 27, Schedule II, was not retrieved. Only the Boeing PDF’s citation of it for Edmonton International runway 12, and that PDF’s reprint of the national table that includes City Centre, were seen.
- No CFS curfew line (the 22:00–07:00 window) was found in the CFS itself. Wikipedia and Flytime state it and attribute the underlying information to Nav Canada or Edmonton Airports. The hours may have been in the CFS; that page was not opened.
- It is not shown whether any CAP/CFS edition after 1984 added a preferential runway or a noise departure procedure beyond 602.106 and the 1978-style rules.

## What did pilots, airlines, and local histories say they were told to do after takeoff?

### Takeaway
Airline and local histories document that jets, especially the Pacific Western 737 shuttle, departed the downtown field for years. They do not record a tower instruction, a heading, or an altitude at which to turn. The one detailed departure track from the field, a 2006 VFR Seneca flight, only says the aircraft was at 8,500 ft ASL nine minutes after takeoff and then tracked about 260° magnetic. No tower-tape description was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Pacific Western 737 operation, as summarized on Wikipedia from airline timetable histories.** Pacific Western flew the “Chieftain Airbus” shuttle between the downtown airport and Calgary with the 737. In 1976 it had up to fourteen 737-200 departures a day nonstop to Calgary, plus direct 737 flights from British Columbia, northern Alberta, and Saskatchewan points. The article says the runway lengths required maximum performance from the 737-200 and that the resulting wear was a concern. It does not describe the departure path. Air Canada DC-9-30s operated briefly in the mid-1980s and left because of field and weight limits. Time Air / Canadian Regional flew Fokker F28s. Echo Bay Mines flew a 727-100 combi for several years. Air BC flew BAe 146-200s as Air Canada Connector. — [Wikipedia, airline section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_City_Centre_Airport)
- **AvCanada, “Edmonton, A Century in the Air” (Tom Hinderks, Alberta Aviation Museum).** Pacific Western’s airbus to Calgary began in 1963 with DC-4s and became more popular in 1968 when 737s entered service, “an airplane designed specifically to get in and out of City Centre’s relatively short runways.” No departure heading. — [AvCanada thread](http://www.avcanada.ca/forums2/viewtopic.php?p=847210)
- **YEG corporate history (about the split between the two airports, not a CYXD procedure).** In the 1970s and 1980s the Municipal Airport was the short-haul hub and “passenger growth at the Municipal Airport created more aircraft noise for city residents.” In October 1995, 77 percent of voters chose to consolidate commercial flights at International; Municipal would continue as the city-centre field for private and general aviation. — [YEG history](https://flyyeg.com/corporate/about-yeg/history/)
- **1982 ECA hearings.** The 737 was named as the main noise source at Municipal, and the recommendation was to consider banning regular large-jet use, not to publish a turn. — [ECA 1982 report](https://nonoise.org/epa%20index/roll12/roll12doc53.pdf)
- **2006 TSB A06W0111.** VFR departure from City Centre; 8,500 ft ASL in nine minutes; enroute track about 260°M toward Prince George. No ATC phraseology and no noise-procedure compliance note. The residential-area avoidance turn in that report is at Edson. — [TSB A06W0111](https://www.tsb.gc.ca/eng/rapports-reports/aviation/2006/a06w0111/a06w0111.html)
- **AvCanada threads on the 2013 closure.** They discuss last departures, the museum 737, and removal of pavement. They do not describe how tower cleared a normal departure. — [Celebrate Blatchford](http://www.avcanada.ca/forums2/viewtopic.php?p=851506); [YXD countdown](http://www.avcanada.ca/forums2/viewtopic.php?t=91617)
- **City auditor, 2004-era access policy (operations policy, not a flight procedure).** After the 1995 consolidation, Edmonton Airports limited scheduled service at City Centre. By 1999, aircraft up to 19 seats were allowed if the carrier kept a 10-passenger cap. From 1 January 2004, aircraft with more than 10 seats could no longer provide scheduled service. The lease still required a 7×24 precision-approach public airport for general aviation, charters, and air ambulance. — [City auditor report](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/assets/PDF/03079_City_Centre_Airport.pdf)

### Inferences
- Pilots of 737s in the 1970s and 1980s were departing this field in large numbers, so some noise practice was in daily use. The written pieces of that practice that were actually found are the 1978 runway-choice and weight rules, the later heavy-turbojet certificate rule, and the reported night noise window. They are not a reconstructed radar heading.
- A north-side monitor at Griesbach (1986) shows the City was measuring Municipal noise on the north side of the field. It does not establish that departures were required to fly north.

### Gaps
- No first-person AvCanada, PPRuNe, or similar account was found that quotes what Edmonton tower or terminal told a CYXD departure to do after takeoff.
- No tower-tape transcript was found.
- Pacific Western, Air Canada, Canadian Regional, and Integra histories that were found give fleets and city pairs, not the noise departure.
- The 1955 Spartan York accident after takeoff from runway 33 was not pulled as a procedure source; it is an accident, not a published departure.
