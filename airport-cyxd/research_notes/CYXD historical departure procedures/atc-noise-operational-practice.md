# ATC, noise, and operational departure practice — CYXD

## Airport identity and era

Blatchford Field (1927) → Edmonton Municipal → Edmonton Industrial → Edmonton City Centre (CYXD/YXD). Closed 30 Nov 2013. User target: a “might have existed” 1940–ca 1980 airfield.

Edmonton International (CYEG) opened 1960; long-haul/jets increasingly moved south. CYXD kept regional/commuter/corporate/medevac. Pacific Western 737 “Airbus” shuttle is documented from 1976 with many daily departures to Vancouver, Prince George, Saskatoon, etc. (Wikipedia, citing airline histories). That is jet departure demand **after** CYEG existed, still from the downtown field.

## How IFR departures actually worked

### 1940s–1950s (radio range / Amber airways)

- Department of Transport **radio range** was the primary IFR track aid. Ranges were calibrated so **one on-course leg fell over a major runway** and opposite legs pointed to adjacent airway stations (radiocom.ca radio-range history).
- **Amber 2** served the Northwest Staging Route (Edmonton–Alaska). A DOT radio operator (Milt Watts, Edmonton Radio, 1955–58) describes a CPA DC-3 **departing Edmonton on Amber 2** for Whitehorse the same evening a USAF C-47 departed **Namao** on Amber 2 for Fairbanks — i.e. Municipal and Namao both injected IFR traffic onto the same airway, with ATC altitude holds for separation.
- Edmonton Radio: range position **with ILS control**, holding NDBs, CPA and later PWA air/ground. This is tower/radio-range/ATC, **not** a published SID.
- Reconstruction for sim (this era): takeoff, climb runway heading, intercept/join the Edmonton range course for the airway in the clearance (Amber 2 northbound typical for Yukon/Alaska), maintain assigned altitude, contact Edmonton Radio / ATC.

### 1960s–1980s (radar terminal, jets, still few graphic SIDs)

- Canadian practice at smaller/busy municipal fields: IFR clearance on the ground (later CLNC DEL), takeoff from tower, **radar identification**, then **vectors or “on course”** to the first airway fix. SIDs were concentrated at major terminals (later CYEG, CYYC, CYYZ).
- No LOA or phraseology transcript for CYXD tower/terminal was found.
- Late frequencies (see official notes) show Edmonton Terminal 119.5 as ARR/DEP — consistent with radar handoff after airborne.

### After 1980 (context only)

- Named SID **CITY FOUR (CYXD4)** appears in procedure indexes; plate not public.
- RNAV approaches added in GNSS era.
- RWY 16/34 closed 3 Aug 2010; last civil departure 30 Nov 2013 (Cessna 172 C-FWKV).

## Noise and preferential use

Documented:

- CAR 602.105/106 as above (all runways noise-restricted for heavy turbojets).
- Curfew-style **strictest** rules 22:00–07:00 local; airport not fully closed overnight.
- Downtown building height ~150 m (flytime.ca / local reporting) — obstacle environment for climb, not a published DP.
- Grokipedia (weak source) claims preferential runway use and climb restrictions; **not verified** against CFS/CAP text.

Not found: NADP 1/2 text for CYXD (those appear for **CYEG**). Do not copy CYEG “no right turns west of KEKBI/Leduc NDB” onto CYXD.

Likely operational bias (inference, not plate): downtown and river valley lie generally **south/southeast** of Blatchford; northbound RWY 34 and northwest RWY 30 take aircraft away from the core. RWY 16 and 12 point more toward the city. Preferential use of 34/30 in a 1960s–80s jet/noise political climate is plausible but **unpublished** in sources reviewed.

## Namao (CYED) and CYEG

- Namao (RCAF/CFB) immediately north: same Amber 2 / later Victor structure; mixed civil/military IFR in the 1950s (Watts).
- CYEG (1960) south of the city: later got full SID/STAR/noise plates. CYXD remained a **downtown Class C-era** satellite with terminal 119.5, not a copy of International SIDs.

## Airlines 1940–1980 (routing, not procedures)

TCA/Air Canada, CPA/CP Air, PWA, Northwest (1950 DC-4 via Edmonton to Anchorage/Tokyo). Timetables show **city pairs**, not SID names.

## VATSIM/IVAO

IVAO Canada CAP 3 index still lists **CYXD Edmonton City Centre** as a charted airport (expired charts for sim). Linked PDFs 404 on the live site. Edmonton FIR (CZEG) manuals describe generic SID phraseology using **CYEG/CYYC** examples, not CYXD.
